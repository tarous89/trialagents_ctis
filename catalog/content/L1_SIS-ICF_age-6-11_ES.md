Template supplied for Spain, in Spanish: **Hoja de información y asentimiento para niños** (Subject Information and Assent Form - Children).

Keep the model text and its order. Fill the [bracketed] notes and the ........... gaps from the study; follow the italic and bracketed instructions and remove them from the final document. Fields completed by hand at the consent visit (names, dates, signatures) stay blank.

**Content instructions:** Prepare a separate child-facing information and assent document for participants aged 6-11. It supplements, and never replaces, the legally effective information and consent provided by the parent(s) or legal guardian(s). Address the child directly using the second-person singular (tú), short sentences, familiar words and explanations adapted to the child's individual age, maturity, health, emotional state and communication needs. The written document supports an age-appropriate oral discussion by a member of the research team experienced in communicating with children. Explain that the parent(s) or guardian(s) give the legal permission, while the child is being asked what they think and whether they affirmatively wish to participate. Silence, passivity or absence of objection must not be treated as assent. A clear refusal, refusal of an individual procedure or later wish to stop must be taken seriously and respected when the child can form and express an opinion. State plainly that participation is voluntary, the child may say no, may ask for more time, may stop at any time without giving a reason, and will not lose ordinary care or be blamed. Rewrite, rather than merely shorten, the decision-critical content of the adult template: why the study is being done and why children are included; what a clinical trial is; the study medicine or treatment; eligibility checks; treatment and follow-up; visits, procedures and duration; random allocation, placebo and blinding when applicable; effects on school, play, food, travel and daily routines; possible benefit and the possibility of no personal benefit; relevant frequent, serious and unknown risks; pain, fear and inconvenience; available alternatives; what happens if participation stops or the study ends; emergency instructions; study contacts; coded use of data and study-required samples; important new information; and access to a plain-language result summary. Distinguish normal care from activities performed only for the study. Do not minimise risk or discomfort, and do not use unexplained terms such as aleatorización, doble ciego, placebo, biomarcador or seudonimización. Keep full legal, insurance, payment, data-protection and parental-authority details in the separate parent/guardian document; include here only short, accurate information that matters to the child. Do not embed consent for optional substudies, pregnancy or infant follow-up, or secondary/future use of data or biosamples; use the dedicated documents. Include pregnancy testing, puberty-related precautions, genetic testing, images/recordings, digital devices or other sensitive procedures only when actually applicable and explain them in age-appropriate language after appropriate discussion with the parent(s) or guardian(s). When the child reaches 12 years during the trial, provide the separate adolescent information/consent document and obtain the additional consent required in Spain before continued participation. Replace all drafting instructions, examples, alternatives and incomplete fields with study-specific information, remove non-applicable content, and retain clear square-bracket placeholders when the protocol does not supply required facts. Provide the child with a copy when appropriate and retain the assent record with the corresponding parental consent documentation.

## Checklist
- C1.01 Study title, protocol code, sponsor and EU trial number are the same as in the application.
- C1.02 The document title clearly identifies the population or use case.
- C1.03 Site and investigator fields are correctly completed or intentionally left as controlled placeholders for site completion.
- C1.04 The introduction states that participation is offered in a research study that has received the required CEIm and AEMPS review, and invites the person to read, ask questions and consult others.
- C1.05 Study purpose, intended population, total planned participant number, treatments, treatment groups and the chance of receiving each option match the protocol; no full inclusion and exclusion list.
- C1.06 States whether the study medicine is authorised and whether its use is within the authorised indication.
- C1.07 Benefits described only when supported; states that the participant may receive no health benefit; free medicine, tests, results and closer follow-up are not presented as benefits.
- C1.08 Main effective treatment alternatives listed, including not joining; says so if the same medicine may be available in normal care.
- C1.09 Treatment after the trial: whether and under what conditions the study treatment may remain available.
- C1.10 Contacts: study doctor name, service, telephone and availability; urgent issues directed to usual or emergency care; participants told to disclose participation elsewhere and carry any trial card.
- C1.11 New information affecting willingness to continue will be communicated promptly; circumstances in which the sponsor or investigator may stop participation and follow-up that continues are explained.
- C1.12 No statements carried over from another protocol, country, cohort or study phase.
- C2.01 Treatment groups, allocation ratio, blinding, placebo, dose, route, schedule and treatment duration match the current protocol.
