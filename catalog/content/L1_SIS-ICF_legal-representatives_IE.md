Template supplied for Ireland, in English: **Trial Information and Consent Form – Legally Designated Representative** (Trial Information and Consent Form - Legal Representative).

Keep the model text and its order. Fill the [bracketed] notes and the ........... gaps from the study; follow the italic and bracketed instructions and remove them from the final document. Fields completed by hand at the consent visit (names, dates, signatures) stay blank.

**Content instructions:** Tailor the document to the specific clinical trial; remove sections and alternatives that do not apply and add study-specific information needed for an informed decision. Use clear Irish English, plain language consistent with National Adult Literacy Agency principles, approximately a 12-year-old reading level where feasible, short paragraphs, descriptive headings and bullets. Spell out abbreviations on first use and explain unavoidable medical or technical terms. Keep the document consistent with the approved protocol, CTIS Part II dossier and Irish site arrangements. Describe research versus usual care, purpose, why the person is invited, procedures in the order experienced, duration, risks and burdens, possible benefits, alternatives where relevant, voluntariness and withdrawal, insurance/injury arrangements, costs/reimbursement, results, contacts and ethics approval. For Irish data protection, identify the personal data used, sources, controllers/processors/recipients, retention, security, medical-record access, transfers outside the EEA and participant-facing safeguards, rights, Data Protection Officer and complaint route; use a simple study-specific explanation of the GDPR lawful basis and include explicit consent for health-research data where applicable. For biological samples used within the main trial, state sample type and amount, whether collection/use is mandatory or optional, purpose, coding, storage location and duration, analyses, recipients/transfers, security and what happens after analysis or withdrawal. For genetic or genomic testing within the main trial, state whether it is mandatory or optional, the type and purpose of testing, limits to anonymity, foreseeable privacy/family risks, handling of individual or incidental findings, storage/security, transfers and any relevant commercial involvement. Required study procedures, required biological samples and required genetic/genomic testing must be clearly described and, when applicable, included in the Required acknowledgements list with an individual confirmation checkbox. Use separate Yes/No choices only for genuinely optional activities that may be refused without preventing the main activity covered by the form. Do not include optional storage or secondary/future research beyond the approved main-trial purposes; that belongs only in the separate Secondary and Future Use template. Use when an adult lacks decision-making capacity and trial participation must be authorised by an Irish legally designated representative. The representative is the addressee; all procedures, risks, benefits, data and samples concern the participant. Under current NREC guidance, a family member or person with a personal relationship who can best interpret the participant’s will and preferences may act; if none is available, a medical practitioner primarily responsible for the participant’s treatment and not involved in the trial may act. The participant must be informed/involved as far as possible and any refusal respected. The representative’s trial-participation consent must not be presented as consent to processing the incapacitated adult’s health-research data; confirm the separate Irish data-processing basis/HRCDC pathway where applicable. If capacity returns, obtain the participant’s own consent promptly.

## S01 · Information for the legally designated representative
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H2 document subline, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>Information for the legally designated representative</b>
Standard wording: <b>Drafting note:</b> In Ireland use the term “legally designated representative”. Do not use “legally authorised representative”, “legally acceptable representative”, “proxy assent” or “next of kin” as substitutes. Confirm outside this participant-facing document that the person acting as representative meets the applicable Irish definition.
Standard wording: You are being asked to decide whether the adult participant you represent may take part in this clinical trial. The participant, not you, is the person taking part. Read this information carefully, ask questions and take the time you need.
Standard wording: The participant should receive information adapted to their ability to understand and should be involved as far as possible. Any clear refusal or wish to stop must be respected.

## S03 · What will happen if you agree?
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H3 module title, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>What will happen if you agree?</b>
Standard wording: Describe the study treatment, comparator/placebo, randomisation/blinding, screening, visits, procedures, duration, sample volumes and follow-up from the participant’s perspective. State what is research versus usual care and what responsibilities, if any, fall on the representative.
Standard wording: The participant’s expressed or observed wishes must be checked throughout. If the participant refuses a procedure, explain whether the study can continue safely or whether participation must end.

Fill: standard

Standard wording: Explain any realistic possible direct benefit to the participant or state clearly if no direct benefit is expected. Do not promise benefit.

Standard wording: Describe foreseeable and unknown risks, side effects, discomforts, practical burdens, reproductive risks and privacy risks. Explain precautions, safety monitoring and what happens if something goes wrong. Include genetic-testing risks when applicable.

## S07 · What will happen to the participant's samples and genetic information?
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H3 module title, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>What will happen to the participant's samples and genetic information?</b>
Standard wording: <b>Drafting note:</b> Include this section whenever biological samples are collected, used or stored for the main trial. Remove only if no biological material is involved.
Standard wording: State exactly which samples are collected or used, including existing clinical samples where applicable, the amount, when and how they are collected, and whether each sample or test is required for the main trial or genuinely optional. Explain the main-trial purpose, coding/pseudonymisation, storage location and duration, laboratories or other recipients, transfers outside the EEA, security, and whether samples are destroyed, used up or retained only for the approved main-trial purpose after analysis.
Standard wording: <b>[Conditional – genetic/genomic testing within the main trial]</b>
Standard wording: Explain the type and purpose of genetic testing in plain language; whether it is required or optional; the fact that DNA is unique and cannot be regarded as completely anonymous; foreseeable privacy, family or discrimination risks; whether individual results or incidental findings may be returned and how; any counselling available; storage/security and transfers of genetic material/data; and any relevant commercial involvement.
Standard wording: Do not include storage or use for unspecified future or secondary research here. Use the separate Secondary and Future Use template for any activity outside the main-trial purposes.

## S08 · What information about the participant will be used?
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H3 module title, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>What information about the participant will be used?</b>
Standard wording: Describe the participant’s personal and health data, sources, relevant medical-record access, coding/pseudonymisation, controllers/processors/recipients, study-specific lawful basis, storage, retention, security, transfers outside the EEA and safeguards. Identify the Data Protection Officer and complaint route.
Standard wording: <b>Drafting note:</b> In Ireland, consent by a legally designated representative to trial participation must not be presented as automatically providing consent for processing the personal data of an adult lacking decision-making capacity. Confirm the appropriate Irish data-processing route, including whether a Health Research Consent Declaration Committee declaration is required, and describe the participant-facing effect without unnecessary legal detail.

- C1.06 Reasonably foreseeable risks, burdens, discomforts, disadvantages and precautions are explained in understandable terms.
- C1.07 Expected benefits are stated, including a clear statement if there may be no personal benefit.

- C1.10 Compensation, reimbursement, insurance, research-injury and complaint arrangements are explained.

- C1.13 New information that could affect willingness to continue will be provided, and this is stated.
- C1.14 The applicable cooling-off or reflection opportunity is given where practicable.
- C1.15 Where participants can find public trial information and results is stated.
- C1.16 Participants are not referred to the protocol, Investigator's Brochure or SmPC to discover material risks; the information needed for consent is in the document itself (IE-ICF).
- C2.01 Visit number, visit length, study duration and follow-up period agree with the protocol.
- C2.02 Procedures, sample volumes, imaging, biopsies and optional substudies agree with the protocol.

- C2.04 Foreseeable risks, burdens, reproductive precautions and expected benefits agree with the protocol and IB or product information.

- C2.07 Pregnancy reporting and follow-up routes agree.

- C3.01 Each consent statement has a corresponding explanation in the information sheet.
- C3.02 Required trial participation is not mixed with optional choices; each optional activity is described before its consent statement.
- C3.03 Optional items have separate initials, tick boxes or signatures and can be declined independently; declining does not block main-study participation unless the activity is genuinely necessary, in which case it is explained and not presented as optional.
- C3.04 Consent to record access identifies the relevant monitors, auditors, regulators and authorised bodies.
- C3.05 Data and sample wording does not promise complete deletion where legal retention duties apply.
- C3.06 Signature blocks cover the participant or representative, the person conducting consent, and date and time where needed.
- C3.07 Guardian, legally designated representative, witness and interpreter blocks appear only in the versions that use them.
- C3.08 No pre-ticked boxes or bundled future-use permission appears.
- C4.01 Every population and inclusion pathway permitted by the protocol maps to a population with an appropriate information and consent route, populations are mutually exclusive within each consent stage, and every population and optional procedure that changes the information or consent logic has the correct document; adults who can consent use the main SIS and ICF. (applies when: always)
- C4.02 Healthy volunteers (IE-HV): information tailored to non-therapeutic participation; no therapeutic benefit claimed (screening, monitoring, payment or access to staff not described as benefit); unknown risk not minimised and prominent for first-in-human or novel-mechanism studies; prior human exposure, escalation, sentinel dosing and stopping rules explained; screening procedures, screening-failure handling and incidental findings covered; visits, confinement, sampling and restrictions complete; reproductive precautions match the protocol; payment itemised, separate from expenses and not contingent on completing every procedure; insurance and emergency contacts correct; repeat-volunteer checks explained; optional samples or future research have separate choices. (applies when: population.healthy_volunteers)
- C4.03 Participants aged 16-17 (IE-MINORS): own informed consent to participation and data processing; no parent or guardian consent required solely because of age; content matches the capable adult version in age-appropriate language. (applies when: population.age_16_17)
- C4.04 Participants under 16 (IE-MINORS): parent or guardian consent document present with the full information, how the child will be informed and how dissent is handled; age-appropriate assent materials for each band the protocol needs, with the bands justified; the protocol age range matches every SIS, ICF and assent version; assent sought according to capacity, age and maturity; refusal and withdrawal respected; re-consent at 16 described; risks and burdens written for both guardian and child; school, family and social impacts addressed where relevant; young children not asked to sign adult legal or data-protection declarations; signature blocks match the actual pathway. (applies when: population.minors)
- C4.05 Adults unable to consent (IE-CAPACITY): accessible participant information plus full representative SIS and ICF; Irish legally designated representative hierarchy followed (personal relationship first, otherwise the treating medical practitioner not involved in the trial); Article 31 conditions addressed; the participant's will, preferences and dissent respected; trial participation consent separated from data-processing safeguards; representative signature block identifies the representative, relationship or role, person taking consent and date; re-consent after capacity returns is operational; no incentive for the incapacitated participant. (applies when: population.adults_without_capacity)
- C4.06 Emergency enrolment (IE-EMERGENCY): used only where the protocol and law allow deferred consent; all Article 35 conditions addressed, not selected individually; post-enrolment participant SIS and ICF explains why enrolment occurred before consent, what has already happened, current risks, alternatives and benefits, continued procedures, the right to refuse continued participation and what happens to data and samples already collected; representative documents and prompt participant re-consent present; the Irish data-processing safeguard is lawful and described. (applies when: consent.emergency_enrolment)
- C4.07 Impartial witness (IE-WITNESS): included only where it may be needed; the participant or representative remains the decision-maker; fields for participant or representative name and mark or signature method, witness name, signature and date, witness confirmation of impartiality and attendance for the full discussion, the reason the witness was used, the person conducting consent, and interpreter details and attestation where a separate interpreter is used; translation and interpreting needs handled separately; the participant receives a copy of the completed consent record. (applies when: consent.impartial_witness_possible)
- C4.08 Pregnancy follow-up (IE-PREGNANCY): participant and partner pathways use separate documents; the pregnant partner consents for herself and the participant does not sign for her; data fields and follow-up duration match the protocol; medical-record and clinician access explicit and specifically permitted; maternal, fetal and infant data distinguished with a defined follow-up endpoint; optional collection separated from required safety reporting; withdrawal and legal retention accurate; contact methods protect confidentiality, with a choice of safe contact method; no language that pressures pregnancy decisions.
