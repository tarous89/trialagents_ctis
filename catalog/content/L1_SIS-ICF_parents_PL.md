Template supplied for Poland, in Polish: **Informacja dla przedstawiciela ustawowego i formularz świadomej zgody na udział dziecka w badaniu klinicznym** (Trial Information and Consent Form - Parents).

Keep the model text and its order. Fill the [bracketed] notes and the ........... gaps from the study; follow the italic and bracketed instructions and remove them from the final document. Fields completed by hand at the consent visit (names, dates, signatures) stay blank.

**Content instructions:** Use this template for the parent(s) or other statutory representative(s) who are legally authorised under Polish law to decide whether a minor child may participate in a clinical trial. Preserve the English taxonomy fields exactly as provided in the project template table. Retain the adult-patient source template's section granularity unless a section is genuinely inapplicable to paediatric participation; do not merge the intervention overview with detailed procedures, adverse effects with broader risks, confidentiality with the formal GDPR notice, benefits with treatment alternatives, reimbursement with insurance/compensation, or the representative consent declaration with the investigator attestation. In Polish participant-facing text, address the individual reader consistently as „Pan/Pani” so the same information can be used by one parent, two parents reading separately, or another statutory representative. Refer to the minor as „dziecko”, „dziecko, które Pan/Pani reprezentuje” or „uczestnik badania”. Avoid „Państwa dziecko”, because the signer may be a court-appointed guardian rather than a parent. The child—not the parent or guardian—is the person receiving the investigational product, undergoing procedures, bearing risks and burdens, potentially receiving benefits, providing data and samples, and requiring follow-up. Rewrite each section rather than mechanically replacing pronouns. Information must be provided by an appropriately qualified member of the study team who is trained or experienced in working with minors. Explain the paediatric rationale and the expected direct benefit to the child or benefit to the represented paediatric population, together with the applicable limitation of additional risk and burden. State early that representative consent is voluntary and may be refused or withdrawn without disadvantage to the child or interruption of ordinary care. Explain separately that every minor must be given information appropriate to age and maturity as far as reasonably possible. The explicit wish of a minor who is capable of forming an opinion and assessing the relevant information to refuse participation or withdraw must be respected and documented by the investigator. Do not describe silence, lack of protest or mere cooperation as agreement. Apply the Polish age rules used in the linked child and adolescent templates: a minor below 13 does not sign a declaration of informed consent; children aged 6–11 receive a separate simplified information-only document; a 12-year-old receives age-appropriate information without a child consent signature; from completion of age 13 until age 18, the minor's own separate written informed consent is required in addition to representative consent. For younger children, provide an individual age-appropriate explanation even where no separate written child document is used. If the child turns 13 during the trial, obtain the separate adolescent informed-consent form before continued participation; if the participant turns 18, use the separate adult SIS/ICF. Do not embed either later consent process as a module or signature block here. Verify and document the identity and authority of every person signing. For a child under parental authority of both parents, participation is an important matter that should result from their joint decision. Finalise one locally accepted documentation route: (A) signatures of both parents; or (B) one parent's signature preceded by the NKB-recommended confirmation that participation was discussed with the other parent, who is also the child's statutory representative, and that they jointly decided to consent. The one-parent route must not be used where the parents disagree; in the absence of agreement, the matter is for the guardianship court. Use a sole-representative block only when one person alone is legally authorised, and record the basis. Remove unused alternatives before finalisation. Do not include an impartial-witness block in this standard parental template; use the dedicated impartial-witness process when the signing representative cannot read, write or use the ordinary consent method. Do not offer inducements, gifts or financial gratification to the child or representative; describe only reimbursement of participation-related expenses and loss of earnings. Pregnancy testing, contraception, reproductive risks and reporting obligations must be included only when applicable to the child's age, development and protocol, and must also be explained directly in the age-appropriate child or adolescent document. Do not include pregnancy-outcome follow-up consent. If the main trial collects biological samples or genetic material or performs molecular/genetic analyses, describe the collection, amount, timing, purpose, analyses, coding, recipients, storage, destruction, risks and return of results in detail. Exclude optional biobanking and secondary/future use; use the separate Secondary Use of Data or Biosamples template. The child remains the data subject and sample donor. Explain separately the limited contact and authority data collected from the representative. Do not present trial participation consent as the legal basis for all GDPR processing. The formal privacy notice must contain all applicable information required by Articles 13 and 14 GDPR, including controllers, DPOs, purposes, legal bases, legitimate interests where applicable, data categories and sources, recipients, international transfers and safeguards, retention, rights and any research-related limitations, complaint rights, whether data provision is required and the consequences of non-provision, automated decision-making/profiling, and information about further processing. The consent form must use first-person representative wording, support singular and plural signers, avoid repeated statements beginning with „rozumiem, że”, identify the child and representative(s), record legal authority, contain the locally applicable parental signature route, include the investigator or delegated consent-taker signature, and confirm that complete signed copies and the appropriate child-facing information were provided.

## Checklist
- C1.01 Current trial title, EU trial number where included, sponsor name and the correct study population.
- C1.02 Study purpose, treatment arms, randomisation, blinding, placebo or comparator use and duration match the protocol.
- C1.03 Visits, procedures, hospital stays, home activities, sampling, imaging and questionnaires are described completely enough for informed consent, and no procedure appears that is not in the protocol.
- C1.04 Numerical details agree with the protocol and schedules: dose schedules, visit frequency, treatment periods, follow-up duration and approximate blood volumes.
- C2.01 The PIS is consistent with the protocol's objectives, interventions, schedule of activities, risk sections, contraception requirements, prohibited medicines, withdrawal rules and follow-up plan.
- C2.02 Risks material to the decision are in understandable language; investigator-facing safety language is translated into its practical meaning for the participant.
- C2.03 Every optional procedure is genuinely optional in the protocol, and refusing it does not exclude the participant unless the protocol requires it.
- C3.01 The ICF records the decisions actually described in the PIS; the main agreement to participate is distinguishable from optional choices (future research, optional genetic work, other activities outside the core protocol).
- C3.02 The signature block supports the Article 29 pathway: the participant or legally designated representative signs and dates, and the person performing the consent interview signs and dates. The blank CTIS submission version is not signed.
- C3.03 Where people unable to write may be enrolled, the form supports the impartial-witness pathway (one witness) rather than adding a second legal representative. (applies when: consent.impartial_witness_possible)
- C4.01 Every population and inclusion pathway in the protocol is mapped to a participant document; the adult ICF is not assumed to cover every situation.
- C4.02 Checked specifically: minors and age-specific information; adults who may lack capacity; emergency enrolment; participants who can consent but cannot write; pregnancy follow-up of a participant; pregnancy follow-up of a pregnant partner; optional secondary or future use of data or samples; healthy volunteers where risk, benefit and payment differ from patient trials.
- C5.01 Age bands match current Polish rules: under 13 the legal representative consents; a minor who has reached 13 also consents (own written consent with the legal representative's consent). (applies when: population.minors)
- C5.02 Parent signature design: where both parents hold parental authority, the joint parental decision is documented by both parents' signatures or by one parent's signature with an attestation that the decision is a joint decision with the other parent; the route is built into the form, not left to the site. (applies when: population.minors)
- C5.03 Age-band labels are unambiguous and follow NKB notation (13-18 years = has reached 13, has not reached 18); no label spans the 13-year threshold. (applies when: population.minors)
- C6.01 The Polish privacy wording matches the real data flow: controller identities, trial-site roles where described, categories of recipients, monitoring and audit access, international transfers, retention and contact routes for data-protection questions.
- C6.02 Withdrawal is not presented as automatically requiring deletion of every item of lawfully collected trial data; the explanation matches the CTR, the GDPR framework and the sponsor's actual legal basis.
- C6.03 Where access to medical records is requested, the document says who may access them and for what trial-related purpose; no vague statement allowing unlimited access.
- C7.01 Each planned sample matches the protocol: what is collected, why, where it may be analysed, how long it may be stored and whether any use continues after the main trial. (applies when: samples.collected or samples.archive_used)
- C7.02 Scientific use outside the protocol is optional and separable from main participation; the withdrawal wording explains what can and cannot be stopped once data have been used or samples anonymised, where that applies. (applies when: samples.future_use_outside_protocol or data.future_use_outside_protocol)

- C9.08 Contact details are current and usable in Poland.
