Template supplied for Poland, in Polish: **Informacja i formularz świadomej zgody na obserwację ciąży partnerki uczestnika badania oraz zdrowia dziecka** (Pregnancy Follow-up and Consent - Partners).

Keep the model text and its order. Fill the [bracketed] notes and the ........... gaps from the study; follow the italic and bracketed instructions and remove them from the final document. Fields completed by hand at the consent visit (names, dates, signatures) stay blank.

**Content instructions:** Prepare a separate, narrowly focused Pregnancy Follow-up and Consent Form for the pregnant partner of a male trial participant when maternal-health, pregnancy-outcome or identifiable post-birth infant-health information will be collected in connection with the participant's protocol-defined paternal exposure. This form supplements the male participant's trial documentation but does not enrol the pregnant partner in the clinical trial. State clearly that she does not receive the investigational product and decides only about the follow-up and use of her own pregnancy-related data described in the form. The male participant cannot consent on her behalf. Before use, adapt or delete every instruction, placeholder, alternative and conditional module. Retain only the relevant paternal exposure, known, suspected or genuinely uncertain reproductive risk, minimum maternal and pregnancy data, actual sources, contact methods, timepoints, end of follow-up, recipients, transfers, retention periods and consent choices. Refusal or withdrawal must not prejudice the pregnant partner, the child, ordinary healthcare or the male participant's participation in the trial. Link the pregnancy information only with the minimum coded trial data necessary to describe the paternal exposure; do not include unrelated information about the male participant's condition, treatment or trial participation. Do not add diagnosis, eligibility, randomisation, ordinary trial visits, treatment alternatives, contraception modules, trial discontinuation, participant insurance, generic adverse-effect lists, genetic testing, biosamples, secondary use, long-term child follow-up or additional examinations unless directly relevant and actually planned. Any additional research procedure, sample collection, genetic testing or secondary use requires its own adequate information and, where required, separate consent. Obtain explicit authorisation before contacting named external healthcare professionals or accessing external medical records. Use the infant section only when identifiable health data will be newly collected after birth and obtain a separate parental or guardian decision after birth. Where both parents hold parental responsibility, adapt the signature method to the ethics-approved Polish process: signatures of both parents, or one parent's signature confirming a joint decision, or the signature of the sole or otherwise independently authorised legal representative. Do not assume that the male participant automatically has parental responsibility or that antenatal consent covers the child's post-birth data. Provide a pregnancy-specific Polish GDPR notice describing the actual controllers, purposes, legal bases, data sources, coded linkage, recipients, access arrangements, international transfers, retention periods, rights and lawful limitations. The partner's signed decision concerns voluntary follow-up and any confidentiality authorisation; do not present informed consent as the sole GDPR legal basis for processing required by law or safety obligations. Use clear lay language and first-person consent declarations; avoid declarations beginning with 'I understand that'. Confirm adequate discussion time, answers to questions, dated signatures of the pregnant partner and the qualified person conducting the consent discussion, and provision of a complete signed copy.

- C1.01 Current trial title, EU trial number where included, sponsor name and the correct study population.
- C1.02 Study purpose, treatment arms, randomisation, blinding, placebo or comparator use and duration match the protocol.
- C1.03 Visits, procedures, hospital stays, home activities, sampling, imaging and questionnaires are described completely enough for informed consent, and no procedure appears that is not in the protocol.

- C2.02 Risks material to the decision are in understandable language; investigator-facing safety language is translated into its practical meaning for the participant.
- C2.03 Every optional procedure is genuinely optional in the protocol, and refusing it does not exclude the participant unless the protocol requires it.
- C3.01 The ICF records the decisions actually described in the PIS; the main agreement to participate is distinguishable from optional choices (future research, optional genetic work, other activities outside the core protocol).
- C3.02 The signature block supports the Article 29 pathway: the participant or legally designated representative signs and dates, and the person performing the consent interview signs and dates. The blank CTIS submission version is not signed.
- C3.03 Where people unable to write may be enrolled, the form supports the impartial-witness pathway (one witness) rather than adding a second legal representative. (applies when: consent.impartial_witness_possible)
