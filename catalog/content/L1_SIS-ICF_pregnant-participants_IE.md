Template supplied for Ireland, in English: **Pregnancy Follow-up Information and Consent Form – Pregnant Participant** (Pregnancy Follow-up and Consent - Participants).

Keep the model text and its order. Fill the [bracketed] notes and the ........... gaps from the study; follow the italic and bracketed instructions and remove them from the final document. Fields completed by hand at the consent visit (names, dates, signatures) stay blank.

**Content instructions:** Use only for pregnancy follow-up of a trial participant who becomes pregnant during the trial or protocol-defined exposure period. This document supplements the original trial consent. Cover only pregnancy-specific purpose and risks, consequences for study treatment, minimum maternal/pregnancy/outcome and applicable infant data, sources and follow-up period, contact with healthcare professionals, relevant biological samples/genetic testing only if actually planned, focused data handling and withdrawal, and applicable consent choices. Include explicit consent for processing health-research data as the default for a capable person; remove it only where an approved Irish alternative or consent-declaration route applies. Required follow-up activities belong in the Required acknowledgements list with an individual confirmation checkbox; use Yes/No only for genuinely optional activities. Do not repeat general trial information or include secondary/future use. Add the conditional under-16 assent/parental-consent block only for trials that accept minors.

## S01 · Pregnancy follow-up information
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H2 document subline, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>Pregnancy follow-up information</b>
Standard wording: <b>Drafting note:</b> Use this adult/capable form for a pregnant person aged 16 or older who can consent. If the pregnant participant is under 16, use an age-appropriate assent plus parent/guardian consent pathway.
Standard wording: You became pregnant during the clinical trial or within the protocol-defined period after study treatment/exposure. This document asks for your consent to collect information about your pregnancy and its outcome for safety follow-up.
Standard wording: This consent is separate from your original trial consent. It does not by itself decide whether you continue or stop the investigational medicine or other main-trial procedures; those decisions follow the protocol and medical assessment.

## S02 · Why are we collecting pregnancy information?
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H3 module title, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>Why are we collecting pregnancy information?</b>
Standard wording: Explain what is known and unknown about the investigational medicine/intervention and pregnancy, why pregnancy outcomes are collected, and how the information contributes to safety evaluation. Use relevant human/non-clinical information only when it helps the pregnant person understand risk.

## S03 · What does the pregnancy mean for the main trial?
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H3 module title, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>What does the pregnancy mean for the main trial?</b>
Standard wording: Explain whether the investigational medicine must be stopped/interrupted, whether unblinding may be needed, what pregnancy-related safety monitoring is required, and what usual clinical care continues. If continued experimental exposure during pregnancy is permitted, explain this separately and ensure the approved protocol supports it.

## S04 · What are the known or possible risks?
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H3 module title, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>What are the known or possible risks?</b>
Standard wording: Describe known, suspected and uncertain risks to the pregnant participant, embryo/fetus and child, and relevant consequences of stopping or continuing study treatment. Explain any additional tests or monitoring and their risks.

## S05 · What information will be collected?
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H3 module title, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>What information will be collected?</b>
Standard wording: List the pregnancy/maternal-health data, relevant investigational-product exposure and concomitant medicines, pregnancy course, outcome and any agreed infant information. State sources: participant, trial records, relevant medical records, obstetrician/maternity hospital or other named healthcare professionals. Access must be limited to information relevant to pregnancy follow-up.

## S06 · How long will follow-up last and who may be contacted?
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H3 module title, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>How long will follow-up last and who may be contacted?</b>
Standard wording: State the exact follow-up period and timepoints. Explain whether the study team will contact the participant directly and whether the obstetrician/midwife/GP/maternity hospital will be contacted.
Standard wording: <b>Drafting note:</b> If the obstetrician or other healthcare professional will be contacted or relevant pregnancy records accessed, include the corresponding explicit consent item below.

## S07 · Will any samples or genetic tests be collected?
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H3 module title, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>Will any samples or genetic tests be collected?</b>
Standard wording: <b>Drafting note:</b> Pregnancy follow-up is often data-only. Remove this section if no biological samples or genetic/genomic testing are collected specifically for pregnancy follow-up.
Standard wording: If samples are collected, state what, how much, why, when, whether required or optional, coding, storage, laboratories/recipients, transfers and what happens after analysis. If genetic/genomic testing is done, explain its type/purpose, whether required or optional, privacy/family risks, incidental/individual findings, counselling, storage/security and transfers. Do not include secondary/future research in this document.

## S08 · What happens to your pregnancy information?
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H3 module title, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>What happens to your pregnancy information?</b>
Standard wording: Explain controllers, purposes, coding/linkage with the participant’s trial data, recipients, relevant direct record access, security, retention, international transfers and safeguards, rights, Data Protection Officer and complaint route. Explain any legally required continued safety processing after withdrawal in simple terms.

## S09 · Is pregnancy follow-up voluntary and can you withdraw?
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H3 module title, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>Is pregnancy follow-up voluntary and can you withdraw?</b>
Standard wording: Participation in pregnancy follow-up is voluntary. Refusal or withdrawal does not reduce ordinary medical care. Explain the effect on the main trial separately. State what happens to already collected pregnancy information and samples and what new collection will stop.

## S10 · What about information about the baby after birth?
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H3 module title, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>What about information about the baby after birth?</b>
Standard wording: If the study collects identifiable health information about the child after birth, describe the exact information and follow-up period and use a separate post-birth parent/guardian consent section or document as required. Do not assume that consent given during pregnancy automatically covers the child’s own post-birth personal data.

## S11 · Costs, reimbursement, results and contacts
Fill: standard
Layout elements: H3 module title, Rich body text
Standard wording: <b>Costs, reimbursement, results and contacts</b>
Standard wording: State any pregnancy-follow-up costs/reimbursement, whether individual or overall safety information will be communicated, sponsor/site contacts, obstetric/urgent contact where appropriate, Data Protection Officer and complaint route.

## Checklist
- C1.01 Trial title, sponsor, phase, purpose and treatment allocation agree with the final protocol and other Part II forms.
- C1.02 The document explains in plain English that the activity is research and that participation is voluntary.
- C1.03 Why the person is invited and how many people will take part are stated.
- C1.04 What will happen, what is experimental and what differs from normal care are explained; research-specific procedures are distinguished from standard care.
- C1.05 The investigational treatment, comparator, placebo and the chance of receiving each arm are explained.
- C1.06 Reasonably foreseeable risks, burdens, discomforts, disadvantages and precautions are explained in understandable terms.
- C1.07 Expected benefits are stated, including a clear statement if there may be no personal benefit.
- C1.08 Relevant alternative treatment or care and the effect of declining are explained.
- C1.09 Pregnancy, breastfeeding, contraception and pregnancy-reporting requirements are explained.
- C1.10 Compensation, reimbursement, insurance, research-injury and complaint arrangements are explained.
- C1.11 What happens if the person stops, and what data and samples may lawfully remain, is explained.
- C1.12 Investigator, study-team, Data Protection Officer, rights, injury and complaint contacts are given.
- C1.13 New information that could affect willingness to continue will be provided, and this is stated.
- C1.14 The applicable cooling-off or reflection opportunity is given where practicable.
- C1.15 Where participants can find public trial information and results is stated.
- C1.16 Participants are not referred to the protocol, Investigator's Brochure or SmPC to discover material risks; the information needed for consent is in the document itself (IE-ICF).
- C2.01 Visit number, visit length, study duration and follow-up period agree with the protocol.
- C2.02 Procedures, sample volumes, imaging, biopsies and optional substudies agree with the protocol.
- C2.03 Randomisation, placebo and blinding are described accurately.
- C2.04 Foreseeable risks, burdens, reproductive precautions and expected benefits agree with the protocol and IB or product information.
- C2.05 Rescue treatment, prohibited medicines and lifestyle restrictions agree.
- C2.06 Early withdrawal and sponsor termination consequences agree.
- C2.07 Pregnancy reporting and follow-up routes agree.
- C2.08 Treatment arms, doses, procedure counts, visit length, contraception, storage, retention, recipients, transfers, injury wording and contacts match the recruitment procedure, Irish data statement, compensation form and sample form (IE-ICF final review).
- C3.01 Each consent statement has a corresponding explanation in the information sheet.
- C3.02 Required trial participation is not mixed with optional choices; each optional activity is described before its consent statement.
- C3.03 Optional items have separate initials, tick boxes or signatures and can be declined independently; declining does not block main-study participation unless the activity is genuinely necessary, in which case it is explained and not presented as optional.
- C3.04 Consent to record access identifies the relevant monitors, auditors, regulators and authorised bodies.
- C3.05 Data and sample wording does not promise complete deletion where legal retention duties apply.
- C3.06 Signature blocks cover the participant or representative, the person conducting consent, and date and time where needed.
- C3.07 Guardian, legally designated representative, witness and interpreter blocks appear only in the versions that use them.
- C3.08 No pre-ticked boxes or bundled future-use permission appears.
- C4.01 Every population and inclusion pathway permitted by the protocol maps to a population with an appropriate information and consent route, populations are mutually exclusive within each consent stage, and every population and optional procedure that changes the information or consent logic has the correct document; adults who can consent use the main SIS and ICF. (applies when: always)
- C4.02 Healthy volunteers (IE-HV): information tailored to non-therapeutic participation; no therapeutic benefit claimed (screening, monitoring, payment or access to staff not described as benefit); unknown risk not minimised and prominent for first-in-human or novel-mechanism studies; prior human exposure, escalation, sentinel dosing and stopping rules explained; screening procedures, screening-failure handling and incidental findings covered; visits, confinement, sampling and restrictions complete; reproductive precautions match the protocol; payment itemised, separate from expenses and not contingent on completing every procedure; insurance and emergency contacts correct; repeat-volunteer checks explained; optional samples or future research have separate choices. (applies when: population.healthy_volunteers)
- C4.03 Participants aged 16-17 (IE-MINORS): own informed consent to participation and data processing; no parent or guardian consent required solely because of age; content matches the capable adult version in age-appropriate language. (applies when: population.age_16_17)
- C4.04 Participants under 16 (IE-MINORS): parent or guardian consent document present with the full information, how the child will be informed and how dissent is handled; age-appropriate assent materials for each band the protocol needs, with the bands justified; the protocol age range matches every SIS, ICF and assent version; assent sought according to capacity, age and maturity; refusal and withdrawal respected; re-consent at 16 described; risks and burdens written for both guardian and child; school, family and social impacts addressed where relevant; young children not asked to sign adult legal or data-protection declarations; signature blocks match the actual pathway. (applies when: population.minors)
- C4.05 Adults unable to consent (IE-CAPACITY): accessible participant information plus full representative SIS and ICF; Irish legally designated representative hierarchy followed (personal relationship first, otherwise the treating medical practitioner not involved in the trial); Article 31 conditions addressed; the participant's will, preferences and dissent respected; trial participation consent separated from data-processing safeguards; representative signature block identifies the representative, relationship or role, person taking consent and date; re-consent after capacity returns is operational; no incentive for the incapacitated participant. (applies when: population.adults_without_capacity)
- C4.06 Emergency enrolment (IE-EMERGENCY): used only where the protocol and law allow deferred consent; all Article 35 conditions addressed, not selected individually; post-enrolment participant SIS and ICF explains why enrolment occurred before consent, what has already happened, current risks, alternatives and benefits, continued procedures, the right to refuse continued participation and what happens to data and samples already collected; representative documents and prompt participant re-consent present; the Irish data-processing safeguard is lawful and described. (applies when: consent.emergency_enrolment)
- C4.07 Impartial witness (IE-WITNESS): included only where it may be needed; the participant or representative remains the decision-maker; fields for participant or representative name and mark or signature method, witness name, signature and date, witness confirmation of impartiality and attendance for the full discussion, the reason the witness was used, the person conducting consent, and interpreter details and attestation where a separate interpreter is used; translation and interpreting needs handled separately; the participant receives a copy of the completed consent record. (applies when: consent.impartial_witness_possible)
- C4.08 Pregnancy follow-up (IE-PREGNANCY): participant and partner pathways use separate documents; the pregnant partner consents for herself and the participant does not sign for her; data fields and follow-up duration match the protocol; medical-record and clinician access explicit and specifically permitted; maternal, fetal and infant data distinguished with a defined follow-up endpoint; optional collection separated from required safety reporting; withdrawal and legal retention accurate; contact methods protect confidentiality, with a choice of safe contact method; no language that pressures pregnancy decisions. (applies when: pregnancy.participant_follow_up_beyond_main_consent or pregnancy.partner_follow_up or pregnancy.infant_follow_up)
- C4.09 Optional future research (IE-SECONDARY): separated from trial participation (see section C6). (applies when: samples.future_use_outside_protocol or data.future_use_outside_protocol)
- C4.10 Optional genetics and other optional substudies (IE-PII-CHECKLIST item 3): a separate or clearly distinct version or consent section where the option changes the information or consent logic. (applies when: genetics.optional_testing or substudy.optional_consent)
- C5.01 Controller and relevant contact details are consistent, including the Data Protection Officer.
- C5.02 Data categories, purposes, legal bases, recipients (Irish and international) and transfers with their safeguards agree.
- C5.03 Retention periods, or the method for setting them, agree and are realistic, with the legal or scientific reason; no open-ended 'as long as necessary' wording without explanation.
- C5.04 The documents distinguish consent to trial participation from the legal basis for processing personal data.
- C5.05 Any reliance on explicit consent as a Health Research Regulations safeguard is described accurately.
- C5.06 A legally designated representative is not asked to give explicit consent to data processing for an adult who lacks capacity.
- C5.07 Any required Health Research Consent Declaration Committee route is identified before submission and described consistently. (applies when: data.hrcdc_declaration_needed or population.adults_without_capacity or consent.emergency_enrolment)
- C5.08 Pseudonymised or coded data are not described as anonymous.
- C5.09 Rights are explained accurately: withdrawal from future activities is possible, legal duties may require retention of data already collected, and the practical result and legal basis are stated.
- C6.01 Sample type, amount, collection method and immediate trial use are clear. (applies when: samples.collected or samples.archive_used)
- C6.02 Storage location, duration, access, coding, transfer and destruction arrangements are clear; return of results and incidental findings and any commercial use are stated. (applies when: samples.collected or samples.archive_used)
- C6.03 Genetic or genomic analysis is stated expressly where relevant. (applies when: samples.collected or genetics.optional_testing)
- C6.04 Optional secondary use is separate from required protocol testing; declining does not affect trial participation or care. (applies when: samples.future_use_outside_protocol or data.future_use_outside_protocol)
- C6.05 Broad consent is limited to a described disease, health or research area; it is not permission for unspecified research. (applies when: samples.future_use_outside_protocol or data.future_use_outside_protocol)
- C6.06 Commercial access, international transfer, storage duration, governance and access controls, recontact and return-of-results policies are explicit. (applies when: samples.future_use_outside_protocol or data.future_use_outside_protocol)
- C6.07 Withdrawal consequences explain what can and cannot be retrieved or destroyed, without promising removal from backups, safety files or records that must be kept by law. (applies when: samples.future_use_outside_protocol or data.future_use_outside_protocol or samples.collected)
- C6.08 Future projects will receive the required ethics, scientific, data-protection and institutional review, and this is stated. (applies when: samples.future_use_outside_protocol or data.future_use_outside_protocol)
- C6.09 Re-consent at 16 and limited-capacity constraints (no representative explicit data consent; an HRCDC declaration cannot authorise unknown future research) are addressed.
