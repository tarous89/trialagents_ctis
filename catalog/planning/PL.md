    How potential participants are identified, the first act of recruitment, who approaches them, and how informed consent is provided and obtained. Every consent pathway in the plan (incapacitated adults, minors, impartial witness, emergency) needs its section completed with the Poland-specific fields. Must reflect the actual Polish workflow.

    Sponsor or applicant document; not signed by participants at CTIS submission. Give it a controlled sponsor version and date.

    National additions to the form: - **v105**: Poland: in Polish, state how each recruitment item (K2) is used and how responses from people who are not eligible are handled; that material shown to potential participants in Poland is in Polish; who makes first contact and how personal data are accessed during pre-screening, consistent with K2; for healthy volunteers, how they are identified and approached.

    - **v108**: Poland: in Polish, state that the Polish PIS and ICF are used; that written consent is dated and signed by the participant (or legally designated representative) and by the person conducting the consent interview on the approved version before enrolment or any trial-specific procedure, except under Article 35; that the participant or representative receives a copy; the PIS/ICF versions and dates referenced (identical to the uploaded L1 documents); that information before consent explains the right to seek a benefit from the Clinical Trials Compensation Fund and the mandatory civil liability insurance of the sponsor and investigator (Patient Rights Ombudsman); that optional choices are separate from main participation; and that trial consent is not presented as the GDPR legal basis for processing.

    - **v110**: Poland, when incapacitated adults are included: in Polish, describe how incapacity for the trial decision is assessed and by whom; how the legally designated representative is identified and how their legal authority under Polish law is verified and documented at the site (family relationship alone is not legal authority; no generic hierarchy of relatives); which Polish documents the representative and the participant receive; how the participant''s understanding and opinion are sought and an explicit refusal or withdrawal is respected; whether and when capacity is reassessed and how personal consent is obtained if capacity returns; how signatures and copies are managed; that only expenses and loss of earnings directly related to participation are compensated.

    - **v114**: Poland, when minors are included: in Polish, state the age groups, which document each group receives and who signs: under 13 the legal representative gives consent and the child receives age-appropriate information without a consent signature; from the 13th birthday until 18 the minor''s own written consent is required together with the legal representative''s consent (NKB good practice); age ranges are written in NKB notation ("13-18 lat" = has reached 13, has not reached 18). Where both parents hold parental authority, state how the joint parental decision is documented (both parents sign, or one parent signs with an attestation that the consent results from a joint decision with the other parent) and how authority is verified where only one person holds it or another legal arrangement applies. State that information is given by staff trained or experienced in working with minors; that an explicit refusal or withdrawal by a minor able to form an opinion is respected (Article 32), with guardianship-court involvement where Polish law provides it if minor and representative disagree; how verbal or behavioural dissent of younger children is handled; that the adolescent form is used when a child reaches 13 during the trial and adult re-consent is obtained at 18; and that optional secondary-use choices are made by the person legally entitled at that age.

    - **v117**: Poland, when an impartial witness may be needed: in Polish, state that a participant or legally designated representative who can decide but cannot write gives consent by an approved alternative means in the presence of at least one impartial witness, who signs and dates the ICF (Article 29 CTR; NKB good practice); that the two-witness rule for certain medical experiments does not apply to CTR trials (Article 29b of the Act on the professions of physician and dentist); how the witness is selected and impartiality supported; that an interpreter is not automatically the witness; and, if planned, the alternative recording method (for example audiovisual recording of oral consent), what the witness observes and how the record is retained and protected.

    - **v121**: Poland, when emergency enrolment is used: in Polish, describe the clinical circumstances that trigger Article 35; who confirms that the participant cannot consent or receive prior information; why the legally designated representative cannot be informed and consent within the therapeutic window; how a known prior objection is checked; how the emergency decision and its timing are documented; which Polish documents are used after the intervention; how consent for continued participation is sought without undue delay (who is contacted, how failed contact attempts are recorded); re-consent if the participant regains capacity; and what happens, including to data already collected, if subsequent consent is refused.

# National rules – Poland

## Authorities and fee

- **competent authority**: URPL (Office for Registration of Medicinal Products, Medical Devices and Biocidal Products); the President of URPL issues the decision. New applications are submitted through CTIS.
- **ethics**: Ethics assessment under the ethics structure set by the Polish Clinical Trials Act of 9 March 2023; it covers the relevant Part I ethical aspects and the Part II aspects and forms part of the basis for the URPL decision. The National Bioethics Committee for Clinical Trials (NKB) publishes the current good practices for its reviewing teams (2026 set; earlier guidance, including on participant remuneration, revoked).
- **compensation fund**: Clinical Trials Compensation Fund, operated through the Patient Rights Ombudsman: sponsor payment for each application for authorisation (from 14 April 2023, including low-intervention trials); participants must be informed of their right to seek a benefit from the Fund.
- **fee**: National application fee under the Clinical Trials Act; proof of payment to the Medical Research Agency account is uploaded in CTIS (NKB). URPL and NKB publish current payment instructions; amounts are not copied here.
- **legal framework**: Regulation (EU) No 536/2014, supplemented by the Polish Clinical Trials Act (consolidated text Journal of Laws 2026 item 2), which sets the national procedure, ethics structure, insurance rules, fees and the Compensation Fund.

## Language rules

- **participant documents**: pl
- **administrative documents**:
  - pl
  - en
- **rule**: The CTR Q&A v7.3 (July 2026) Annex II marks Poland as requiring a national-language version of the protocol synopsis, patient-facing documents submitted in Part II, labelling and relevant CTIS application fields. Every PIS, ICF and other participant-facing material is in Polish at submission. Administrative EU templates may be completed in English; then check whether participant-facing content is embedded in them and whether a Poland-specific field needs Polish.
- **form answers**: Part II forms (shared EU templates in content/eu/templates/part-ii) are answered in Polish, matching the Polish variable sheet supplied with CTIS_PL; English is possible for administrative documents (DECISIONS.md D1).
- **communication support**: Inability to write is not inability to understand Polish; language or communication support needed for another reason is addressed in addition to the witness route. The guides set no further national rule for people who do not understand Polish.

## Populations, age bands and representatives

- **authority**: NKB (National Bioethics Committee for Clinical Trials) current good practices for reviewing teams (2026 set; predecessor guidance revoked), Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 Articles 28 to 35, the Polish Act on clinical trials of medicinal products for human use of 9 March 2023 (consolidated text Journal of Laws 2026 item 2), Article 29b of the Act on the professions of physician and dentist, and the Patient Rights Ombudsman's information for sponsors, as recorded in the Poland guides reviewed 23 September 2026.
- **minors**:
  - **age bands**:
    - **band**: 6-11
    - **template**: PL-PT-age-6-11
    - **reader**: child
    - **consent**: legal representative only; child information without signature
    - **band**: 12
    - **template**: PL-PT-age-6-11
    - **reader**: child
    - **consent**: legal representative only; age-appropriate information without a child consent signature (parents template rule)
    - **note**: No supplied template is drafted for 12-year-olds; default pending DECISIONS.md D2.
    - **band**: 13-17
    - **template**: PL-PT-age-13-17
    - **reader**: adolescent
    - **consent**: minor's own written consent together with the legal representative's consent
    - **nkb notation**: 13-18 lat (has reached 13, has not reached 18)
  - **always**:
    - PL-PT-parents
  - **threshold**: NKB: under 13 the legal representative gives consent; from the 13th birthday until 18 consent of both the minor and the legal representative is required. The supplied adolescent template was labelled 12-17; the authority's threshold of 13 wins.
  - **band notation**: NKB: a range written as 13-18 years means the participant has reached 13 but has not yet reached 18. Polish document labels use this notation; file names in this repository use inclusive ranges (13-17 = 13th to 18th birthday).
  - **joint parental decision**: Where both parents hold parental authority, participation is an important matter for a joint parental decision (NKB). Document it either by both parents signing the ICF or by one parent signing with an attestation that the consent is the result of a joint decision with the other parent. Build one route into the parent ICF (DECISIONS.md D4). Where one person alone holds authority or another legal arrangement applies, verify the actual legal authority before consent.
  - **disagreement**: The representative's signature does not override the minor; guardianship-court involvement applies under Polish law, and an explicit refusal of a minor able to form an opinion is respected (Article 32).
  - **reconsent at 13**: PL-PT-age-13-17 before continued participation when a child reaches 13 during the trial (template instructions; follows the NKB threshold).
  - **reconsent at 18**: PL-PT-main (or PL-PT-healthy) before continued participation when the participant reaches 18 (Article 32(3)); track birthdays.
  - **under 6**: No template. Involve the child in a way adapted to age and maturity and record their response; dissent is respected (DECISIONS.md D16).
  - **assent signature**: No legally binding child signature under 13. The guide allows an assent signature as an ethical record only if labelled as assent and kept distinct; the supplied child template excludes any signature or assent form, and is used as supplied.
  - **payment**: Only compensation for expenses and loss of earnings directly related to participation.
- **adults without capacity**:
  - **always**:
    - PL-PT-legal-rep
    - PL-PT-limited-capacity
  - **note**: Article 31: consent of the legally designated representative before enrolment (unless Article 35 applies) and information adapted to the participant's capacity, which is an active, mandatory part of the process. The representative's legal authority under Polish law is verified and documented; family relationship alone is not authority. Capacity reassessment and personal consent when capacity returns (PL-PT-main). A combined participant and representative document is acceptable only if it stays clear who is informed, who consents and which signature records which decision.
- **emergency**:
  - **always**:
    - PL-PT-emergency
  - **conditional**:
    - **PL-PT-legal-rep**: incapacity may continue
    - **PL-PT-limited-capacity**: participant can understand some information but cannot yet decide
    - **PL-PT-main**: re-consent when the participant regains capacity
  - **note**: Article 35 conditions all met; consent for continued participation sought without undue delay; refusal of subsequent consent is not overridden by the first intervention.
- **impartial witness**:
  - **always**:
    - PL-PT-witness
  - **note**: One impartial witness (Article 29 CTR; NKB). The two-witness rule for medical experiments does not apply. Trigger: the person giving consent cannot write.
- **pregnancy**:
  - **participant**: PL-PT-pregnancy when follow-up goes beyond the main ICF
  - **partner**: PL-PT-pregnant-partner whenever identifiable partner health information or records are collected
- **shareable templates**:
  - PL-PT-pregnant-partner
  - PL-PT-secondary
- **shareable note**: Shared across populations only when nothing in the document would differ. The secondary-use document must follow the signer route of each population (participant, parent, legal representative), so it is shared only among populations with the same signer route.
- **separate document rules**:
  - Do not combine distinct consent populations into one form if that makes the signature logic unclear; parent, adolescent and adult documents are separate controlled documents where the consent-givers differ.
  - Optional secondary or future use (Article 28(2)) is kept unmistakably separate from main participation; the supplied templates put it in its own form (PL-PT-secondary).
  - A separate optional genetic or genomic research choice where its scope differs materially (DECISIONS.md D14).
  - A pregnant partner receives a separate partner-facing information and consent document; the participant cannot consent for the partner.
  - A separate pregnancy follow-up document when pregnancy triggers contacts, record access or outcome and infant follow-up beyond the main ICF.
  - Emergency enrolment: Polish documents for continued participation after the intervention; never an unsigned 'emergency consent form' presented as prior consent.

## Drafting rules

- All participant-facing documents for Poland are in Polish and prepared before submission; translation and linguistic QC are part of the dossier, not a site-start activity. Review the Polish text clinically, not only linguistically.
- The PIS covers, where applicable: purpose and why the person is invited; procedures, randomisation, blinding, placebo or comparator; foreseeable risks and inconveniences including reproductive risks; expected benefits or a clear statement that no direct benefit is expected; alternatives; duration and follow-up; voluntariness, refusal and withdrawal without detriment; care after withdrawal; confidentiality, personal data, medical-record access and recipients; samples, storage and future use; reimbursement and payments; injury compensation and insurance in Poland; contacts; when the investigator or sponsor may stop participation. Describe what the protocol does rather than reproduce protocol wording.
- Every participant document (main, healthy-volunteer, parent, adolescent, legal-representative, emergency, witness) explains the right to seek a benefit from the Clinical Trials Compensation Fund and the mandatory civil liability insurance of the sponsor and investigator, using the Patient Rights Ombudsman's recommended wording as reference; a generic insurance sentence is not equivalent. For healthy volunteers keep it separate from study payment. In pregnant-partner forms do not suggest the partner has trial-participant status under the scheme.
- Keep the main participation decision separate from optional choices (future use, genetic research, recontact); make the consequence of saying no clear.
- Signatures at consent (Article 29): the participant or legally designated representative and the person conducting the consent interview sign and date; the CTIS copy is blank; the participant receives a copy. Signature lines only for consent-givers and the consent-taker (and the impartial witness where that route applies).
- Impartial witness: one witness (CTR, NKB); never two witness lines; the witness statement covers only what the witness observes.
- Minors: NKB 13-year threshold; joint parental decision route; age bands written in NKB notation; no legally binding signature for children under 13.
- Legally designated representative: describe and verify legal authority; never a generic family-member signature line.
- Payments: minors, incapacitated and pregnant or breastfeeding participants receive only compensation for expenses and loss of earnings; for healthy volunteers separate reimbursement from payment, explain prorating on withdrawal, and do not rely on the NKB remuneration guidance revoked in 2026. Amounts identical to P1.
- Data protection: GDPR together with the Polish Act of 10 May 2018 on the protection of personal data; trial consent is not presented as the GDPR legal basis; withdrawal does not imply deletion of all lawfully collected data; medical-record access names who may review and why; transfers outside Poland or the EEA described specifically.
- Give risks with the frequency categories in the Polish templates (bardzo często, często, niezbyt często, rzadko), matching the IB or SmPC; the Poland guides set no categories of their own.
- Keep defined terms consistent across PIS, ICF, recruitment material, pregnancy and future-use forms; version numbers and dates consistent with K1 and across CTIS.
- Participant templates (JSON): italic text, coloured instructions (blue instructions, green examples, yellow comprehension questions, per each template's styling_instructions), examples marked "Przykład", [square-bracket] notes and gaps are drafting instructions; follow and remove them, together with the section "Informacje i instrukcje ogólne" and the ABM and PSBK logos. Part II forms (DOCX): yellow [INSERT/IF/OTHERWISE/CHOOSE/NOTE: …] labels. Nothing of either may remain in a final document.
