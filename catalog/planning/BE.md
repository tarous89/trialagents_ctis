    How potential participants are identified, approached, informed, given time to decide and consented, with every special pathway (minors, legal representatives, emergencies, witnesses, electronic consent). Where an external platform or call centre recruits or prescreens: its role, privacy policy, staff qualifications, consent and data handling (BAREC and CT-College advice, endorsed 3 August 2026: site involvement, a clear benefit, trained staff who speak the participant''s language, referral to the treating site when appropriate).     National additions to the form: - **v105**: Required in Belgium: state that every advertisement and recruitment item is submitted under K.60 in its final wording and presentation, in at least the official language or languages of the region where it is used, and is not promotional and exerts no undue influence. If an external platform or call centre recruits or prescreens (recruitment.external_platform, recruitment.call_centre): describe its role, privacy policy, staff qualifications, consent and data handling, the site''s involvement, the clear benefit, trained staff who speak the participant''s language and referral to the treating site when appropriate (BAREC and CT-College advice, 3 August 2026); study-specific prescreening data are collected only with a signed consent form. For pre-recruitment by an early-phase unit (recruitment.pre_recruitment): follow the CT-College recommendation (24 January 2025); pre-recruitment does not become trial-specific consent or trial conduct before authorisation; describe databases or panels and repeat-volunteer and safety-database checks.

    - **v108**: Required in Belgium: the consent interview (Article 29 of Regulation 536/2014) is conducted by a physician (a dentist for a dental trial), who may delegate supporting nursing activities but remains responsible and in control (Law of 7 May 2017); the interview is in person or by video conference, never by telephone alone, and the ICF states which format is used; the participant (or legally designated representative) and the person who conducted the interview sign and date the approved form before any trial-specific procedure; the participant receives a copy. State that the current CT-College ICF template is used for adult patients and adult healthy volunteers (sponsor statement if it is not used or mandatory text is changed) and that participant documents are provided in the official language or languages of each site''s region. For electronic consent (consent.electronic): the electronic presentation contains at least the information of the paper ICF, participants can refuse it and use paper, the interview occurs first, the investigator signs last, the signed document is locked with its audit trail preserved and a printed or locked electronic copy is given; a site may refuse electronic information or signing (Belgian guidance on electronic informed consent v2.0, 29 June 2022).

    - **v110**: Required in Belgium when incapacitated adults are included: the legally designated representative is determined by the Law of 7 May 2017 with Articles 12 and 14 of the Patients'' Rights Act, in this order: (1) a representative designated by the adult in a specific written, dated and jointly signed mandate; (2) a court-appointed administrator authorised to exercise the relevant patient rights; (3) a cohabiting spouse, legal cohabitant or de facto cohabiting partner; (4) in order, an adult child, a parent or an adult sibling; (5) if nobody acts or representatives conflict, the healthcare professional safeguards the patient''s interests, where appropriate through multidisciplinary consultation. Describe how authority is confirmed and documented, how capacity is assessed and reassessed (diagnosis, disability, temporary confusion or supported communication do not by themselves remove capacity), the adapted information to the participant, how a prior refusal and an explicit objection are respected, and the participant''s own consent without undue delay once capable.

    - **v114**: Required in Belgium when minors are included: the legally designated representative is the person exercising parental authority or the legal guardian (Law of 7 May 2017, Article 11; Patients'' Rights Act, Article 12). The parent ICF carries two parent signature lines and both parents'' written consent is sought (BAREC and CT-College advice v3.0, 10 July 2026); signing need not be simultaneous. Describe the single-parent exception (other parent deceased, incarcerated, unreachable or truly estranged with no indication of disagreement; not mere absence, separation or divorce), the reasonable efforts to involve both parents, asking the signing parent whether the other agrees, documenting whether the parent signs personally or on behalf of both and the justification; a known refusal by the other parent means no enrolment. Belgium sets no numerical assent age: the investigator assesses discernment case by case (age, maturity, impact of the intervention, mental state, personality, family and social environment, education) and actively seeks and documents assent or dissent; a capable minor''s refusal or wish to withdraw is respected. Describe the age-adapted documents planned, renewed assent as understanding develops, and the participant''s own consent on reaching 18.

    - **v117**: Required in Belgium when an impartial witness may be used: only for a participant who can decide but cannot write, after supporting the participant''s own communication (accessible formats, assistive technology, reading aloud, an authorised alternative mark); not for a language difference, which needs information in a language the participant understands and a competent interpreter. Describe how the witness''s independence is checked and documented (an adult independent of the trial, not part of the investigator team, not controlled by the sponsor, not financially dependent on enrolment, not the substitute decision-maker); justify any relative or support person proposed. The physician (or dentist) conducts the interview and confirms capacity; the method used to record the participant''s consent is documented; the witness signs and dates the attestation after observing the process; the interviewer signs and dates the ICF; the participant receives a signed and dated copy in an accessible form; the site keeps the original and documentation of the witness''s identity and independence (Law of 7 May 2017, Article 12/1).

    - **v121**: Required in Belgium when emergency enrolment is used: every Article 35 condition (sudden life-threatening or other sudden serious condition; no prior information or consent possible; no legally designated representative available within the therapeutic window; no known prior objection; scientific grounds for direct clinically relevant benefit; minimal risk and burden compared with standard treatment) and the defined therapeutic window. Describe the emergency trigger, capacity assessment, search for a representative under the Belgian hierarchy, check for prior objections, eligibility decision, the initial intervention and who decided, notification, and later information and consent without undue delay (representative when available first; the participant''s own decision once capable); refusal stops further trial interventions; the minor safeguards of Article 32 apply where minors may be enrolled. Emergency clinical care is kept separate from the research intervention, and no form is written as if consent preceded enrolment.

# National rules – Belgium

## Authorities and fee

- **competent authority**: FAMHP (Federal Agency for Medicines and Health Products): Belgian national competent authority. Publishes the List of requested documents in CTIS for Belgium (30 June 2026) and the Belgium CTR dossier requirements (updated 30 June 2026).
- **ethics**: CT-College (Clinical Trial College): independent college that assigns a recognised ethics committee and coordinates the ethics review; publishes the national ICF templates, guidance and advice. The assigned ethics committee reviews the ICF in one language. BAREC (Belgian Association of Research Ethics Committees) issues joint advice with the CT-College (patient-facing documents, paediatric signatures, pregnancy follow-up, lifting traceability of samples, external recruitment platforms).
- **procedure**: The sponsor submits one dossier through CTIS and receives the Belgian decision through the same system.
- **fee**: No proof of fee payment at CTIS submission. Belgium invoices applicable safety follow-up and inspection fees separately (BE-PII-CHECKLIST#not-required).
- **law**: Belgian Law of 7 May 2017 on clinical trials of medicinal products for human use (consolidated through 4 June 2026); Belgian Patients' Rights Act of 22 August 2002 (Articles 12 and 14, representatives); Law of 7 May 2004 on experiments on the human person (pregnant-partner follow-up, liability and insurance); Belgian human biological material (biobank) legislation.
- **insurance**: No-fault sponsor liability; insurance required before the trial begins. The certificate states the insured amount and refers to applicable national legislation.

## Language rules

- **participant documents**:
  - fr
  - nl
  - de
- **administrative documents**:
  - fr
  - nl
  - de
  - en
- **rule**: The application dossier may be in Dutch, French, German or English. Participant information, informed consent forms and recruitment materials must be written in the language or languages of the participants and submitted at least in the official language or languages of the region where the trial will run (BE-HUB, BE-ICF).
- **when dutch**: Dutch (nl) for participants recruited in the Dutch-speaking region (Flanders), and for Dutch-speaking participants at Brussels sites.
- **when french**: French (fr) for participants recruited in the French-speaking region (Wallonia), and for French-speaking participants at Brussels sites.
- **when german**: German (de) for participants recruited in the German-speaking region. The CT-College template package is published in Dutch, French and English only: for recruitment in the German-speaking region confirm the expected implementation of the mandatory template with FAMHP or the CT-College before submission and document the translation approach (DECISIONS.md D4).
- **brussels**: Plan French and Dutch versions when both language groups may be recruited; a site-specific recruitment plan may justify the exact set (study fact population.brussels_language_groups; DECISIONS.md D2).
- **translations**: The sponsor is responsible for accurate, matching translations, even though the assigned ethics committee reviews the ICF in one language. Use the current CT-College source text for each official language and do not re-translate its standard paragraphs; translate only the study-specific content. Risk frequencies and time periods keep the same meaning across the Dutch, French, German and English versions.
- **privacy information**: Participant-facing privacy information must be available in the same regional languages as the ICF.
- **endpoint instruments**: Endpoint-related participant instruments belong with the protocol in Part I: multinational trials submit only the English version; mono-national trials may submit a Member State language version in Part I when no English version exists (FAMHP list, June 2026).
- **staff language**: External recruitment platforms and call centres use trained staff who speak the participant's language (BAREC and CT-College advice, August 2026).
- **form answers**: Shared EU forms (content/eu/templates/part-ii) are answered in French, Dutch, German or English. Default: the reference package language (DECISIONS.md D1).

- **minors**:
  - **age bands**:

## Drafting rules

- Participant documents in the language or languages of the participants and at least the official language or languages of each site's region (nl Flanders, fr Wallonia, de German-speaking region, fr and nl in Brussels as the population requires); each language version has the same meaning, and the sponsor is responsible for translations.
- CT-College templates (adult patients, healthy adults; vaccine template recommended) for new initial interventional medicinal-product trials from 1 August 2025: keep fixed text and structure, use the CT-College source text of each official language, translate only study-specific content, follow template instructions where a topic does not apply rather than silently deleting safeguards; sponsor statement with justification when the template is not used or mandatory wording changes. Confirm the template version is current on the day of submission.
- The information lets an adult decide without the protocol: purpose and why invited; product, randomisation, placebo and blinding; visits, tests, procedures, duration and follow-up; foreseeable and unknown risks and burdens; no guaranteed benefit and alternatives; pregnancy, contraception, breastfeeding and partner precautions; samples and data, recipients and retention; optional choices separately; confidentiality and coded data; reimbursement, compensation, injury and Belgian no-fault insurance; voluntariness, withdrawal and continued safety follow-up; contacts for trial questions, urgent issues, participant rights and data protection.
- Write for the participant: define medical terms, explain probabilities understandably, short sections, risks specific to the intervention, no investigator-facing lists without context. Do not promise benefit or describe an experimental product as safe or effective.
- Compensation and reimbursement match the financial documents (P1, financing statement, site agreement); sample wording matches the protocol and S1; insurance wording matches the certificate.
- Privacy: distinguish consent to participate from the legal basis for processing; never call coded data anonymous; identify controllers, recipients, retention, international transfer safeguards, rights and their limits, and data-protection contacts; include the GDPR confirmation in the ICF or file R1.
- Consent interview by a physician (dentist for a dental trial), in person or by video conference, never telephone alone; the ICF states the format. Blank, unsigned, version-controlled ICFs in CTIS; signature and date fields for every person whose consent, assent, attestation or authorisation is required, left blank.
- Electronic consent: at least the paper ICF's information; screens, images, videos and process submitted as seen plus a printable PDF; paper route available; investigator signs last; locked copy for the participant.
- Healthy volunteers: no therapeutic benefit; payment linked to time and burden, not completion or tolerance of risk; withdrawal not disproportionately penalised; neutral recruitment claims.
- Minors: two parent signature lines; age-adapted information for the actual population, no invented national age bands; assent and dissent documented; re-consent at 18.
- Recruitment material and other patient-facing documents: final wording and presentation; not promotional, no undue influence; incentives disclosed.
- Version, date, population and language on every item; version and date in CTIS structured data, not the filename; Annex I letter and language in the filename where practical (for example L Main ICF_NL). Remove signatures, personal addresses and other unnecessary personal data from documents for publication. Tracked-change versions with RFI responses and substantial modifications.
- No participant templates were supplied: once the CT-College templates and the other population templates are supplied they are split into templates/participant per language (*_BE_fr.json, *_BE_nl.json, *_BE_de.json); until then documents cannot be drafted from a template (DECISIONS.md D5).
- Part II templates (DOCX): yellow [INSERT/IF/OTHERWISE/CHOOSE/NOTE: ...] labels; participant templates (JSON, when supplied): italic text, [square-bracket] notes and "..........." gaps. Nothing of either may remain in a final document.
