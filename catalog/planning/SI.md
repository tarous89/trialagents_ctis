    How potential participants are identified, approached, informed, given time to decide and consented. Complete the Member State fields for Slovenia. Every population pathway in the plan (minors with the 15-year threshold and justified age and maturity groupings, adults unable to consent, impartial witness, emergency) needs its section completed; the age and maturity logic for minors is documented here.

    National additions to the form: - **v105**: Required in Slovenia: in Slovenian, state that every recruitment advertisement, invitation, social-media text, video script, call-centre script or other material that participants will see or hear is submitted under Recruitment arrangements in its final Slovenian version (non-print content as final-form text or a sufficiently complete representation) before use; that recruitment claims match the approved Participant Information Sheet and the financial document and payment is not the dominant message; and, for healthy volunteers, how recruitment advertisements and screening invitations are used and what data are collected and retained at a brief eligibility contact.

    - **v108**: Required in Slovenia: in Slovenian, state that the Participant Information Sheet and Informed Consent Form are provided in Slovenian; that a suitably qualified member of the trial team conducts the consent interview, allows sufficient time and confirms understanding rather than relying only on a signature; that the participant or legally designated representative and the person conducting the interview personally sign and date the consent record, signed in the investigator''s presence and prepared in two copies, one for the participant and one for the trial archive (KME RS guidance); that the participant receives the written information and a signed and dated copy; that an interpreter may support comprehension but does not replace an impartial witness or a legally designated representative; that genuinely optional activities have separate statements and signature boxes; that consent to take part in the trial is not treated automatically as the GDPR legal basis for data processing (Information Commissioner); where teratogenic risk exists, that women of childbearing potential, and healthy female partners of male participants where relevant, sign an acknowledgment of the written pregnancy, fetal-risk and contraception information (KME RS guidance, point 12); and how re-consent is obtained after material new information, at adulthood, after regained capacity and for a newly proposed optional use.

    - **v110**: Required in Slovenia when adults unable to consent are included: in Slovenian, describe who assesses capacity, when it is reassessed and how fluctuating capacity is managed (capacity is decision-specific; inability to read or write, a physical disability or the need for an interpreter is not incapacity); how the legally designated representative is identified and their documented legal authority verified (Patients'' Rights Act, Article 37; KME RS guidance refers to a close person or legal representative, but an informal family relationship does not replace documented legal authority where the CTR requires a legally designated representative); that where no representative exists the healthcare provider informs the competent authority so that one can be appointed; that the representative decides according to the participant''s presumed wishes and interests; how the participant receives information suited to their understanding and how assent, cooperation, refusal or dissent is recorded and respected; how each Article 31 condition is met; and the participant''s own express consent when capacity is regained.

    - **v114**: Required in Slovenia when minors are included: in Slovenian, state that the legally designated representative gives the legally effective trial consent for every participant under 18; that under the Patients'' Rights Act (Article 35) a child under 15 is presumed unable, and a child aged 15 or older presumed able, to consent to healthcare unless the doctor assesses otherwise, and that KME RS guidance requires the own agreement of invited minors aged 15 or older in addition to the consent of the parents or representative; explain and justify the age and maturity groupings below 15 and their documents (no fixed national bands below 15); how legal authority for the particular child and family circumstances is verified (a person accompanying the child is not automatically authorised); that information is given by a person trained or experienced in working with children; how assent and agreement are recorded; that an explicit refusal or wish to withdraw of a minor capable of forming an opinion is respected; and the participant''s express informed consent on reaching 18.

    - **v117**: Required in Slovenia when an impartial witness may be needed: in Slovenian, state that a witness is used only when the participant or legally designated representative can consent but cannot write (Article 29 CTR), and not because of an interpreter need, limited vision that an accessible signature method solves, lack of capacity, an unavailable representative or to shorten the interview; how a witness independent of the trial team is selected (not the interviewer or recruiter, no direct financial interest in enrolment, close family avoided where dependence or conflict could undermine impartiality); that the witness is present for the whole information and consent process; whether the approved procedure lets an interpreter also act as witness, with each role documented; and that the reason the person cannot write, the witness''s name and role, and the date and time are recorded.

    - **v121**: Required in Slovenia when emergency enrolment is used: in Slovenian, explain each Article 35 condition for the specific emergency condition and therapeutic window; why prior consent of the participant and representative cannot be obtained; how prior objections and advance information are checked; the intervention permitted before consent; who makes and records the enrolment decision and what is recorded; the later information and consent, without undue delay, of the participant or legally designated representative, and the participant''s own consent when capacity returns; handling of refusal, withdrawal, data and samples and the right to object to the use of data; site training, round-the-clock availability and oversight; and that no Slovenian national emergency-consent form or waiver applies and the KME RS general urgent-action process is not a substitute for the CTIS-approved pathway.

# National rules – Slovenia

## Authorities and fee

- **competent authority**: JAZMP (Javna agencija Republike Slovenije za zdravila in medicinske pripomočke, Agency for Medicinal Products and Medical Devices): validation, scientific assessment, national coordination and notification of the decision.
- **ethics**: KME RS (Komisija Republike Slovenije za medicinsko etiko, Commission of the Republic of Slovenia for Medical Ethics) is the competent ethics committee for the Clinical Trials Regulation in Slovenia and prepares the Part II assessment. A separate pre-submission ethics opinion is not a substitute for the CTIS assessment.
- **fee**: The sponsor pays one combined national procedure fee to JAZMP under the current JAZMP tariff; JAZMP allocates the KME RS portion. Follow the current JAZMP payment instructions for the application type and retain proof of payment. No second standalone KME RS payment for a CTIS application unless an authority instruction for the specific procedure expressly requires it (SI-FEE).
- **national guidance**: Regulation implementing the EU Clinical Trials Regulation, Official Gazette RS No 132/2022 (Articles 2, 5 to 10 and 14 to 15); Patients' Rights Act, Official Gazette RS No 15/2008 (Articles 35 and 37); KME RS Instructions for Preparing Applications for Ethical Assessment, 3 March 2020 (points 10 to 13); KME RS Form of Voluntary and Informed Consent After Information (one-page model, reference only); KME RS official clinical-trial page (updated 21 April 2026); Information Commissioner opinion on clinical trials and data-protection legal bases; European Commission Q&A on Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 (July 2026); EudraLex Volume 10 Part II templates (version 2.0 transition effective 1 September 2026); as recorded in the Slovenia guides reviewed 23 September 2026.

## Language rules

- **participant documents**: sl

- **rule**: The national implementing regulation allows the application dossier in Slovenian or English with three exceptions: the cover letter must be in Slovenian; a Slovenian protocol synopsis with the nationally specified short description is mandatory (current EU language guidance calls for both English and Slovenian synopsis versions in a multinational application); and every document intended for participants must be in Slovenian (PIS, ICF, recruitment material, participant cards, instructions, questionnaires completed by participants, diaries, age-specific information, legal-representative documents and optional-consent materials).
- **translation quality**: Natural Slovenian terminology and short sentences; technical terms defined; a literal translation is not adequate without clinical and linguistic review; the Slovenian text is reviewed by a competent native-language clinical reviewer and a traceable translation and review record is kept.

## Populations, age bands and representatives

- **authority**: KME RS (national ethics committee, Part II assessment) and JAZMP; Regulation implementing the EU Clinical Trials Regulation (Official Gazette RS No 132/2022, Articles 5, 7, 10 and 14); Patients' Rights Act (Official Gazette RS No 15/2008, Articles 35 and 37); KME RS Instructions for Preparing Applications for Ethical Assessment, 3 March 2020 (points 10 to 13); Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 Articles 28 to 35; as recorded in the Slovenia guides reviewed 23 September 2026.
- **template status**: missing: no Slovenian participant templates were supplied. Every template id below is a planned id from participant_templates_missing, with no file ("template": "missing" in population_sets).
- **minors**:
  - **age bands**:
    - **band**: under 15 (groupings defined per study)
    - **template**: SI-PT-children
    - **template status**: missing
    - **reader**: child
    - **basis**: Patients' Rights Act, Article 35: a child under 15 is presumed unable to consent to healthcare unless the doctor considers the child sufficiently mature. No fixed national leaflet bands below 15 were identified in current JAZMP or KME RS CTIS guidance; choose defensible age and maturity groupings and explain them in K1 (DECISIONS.md D15).
    - **condition**: child can understand and express a view
    - **band**: 15-17
    - **template**: SI-PT-adolescents-15-17
    - **template status**: missing
    - **reader**: adolescent
    - **basis**: Patients' Rights Act, Article 35 (a child aged 15 or older is presumed able to consent to healthcare unless the doctor finds otherwise) and KME RS guidance, point 11 (invited minors aged 15 or older provide their own agreement in addition to parent or representative consent). The legally designated representative still gives the legally effective trial consent until 18.
  - **always**:
    - SI-PT-parents
  - **band conflict**: None with templates (none supplied). The national healthcare-consent presumption at 15 and the CTR are combined, not in conflict: representative consent for every minor, plus the minor's own agreement from 15.
  - **representatives**: Confirm who has legal authority for the particular child under Slovenian law and the family circumstances; a person accompanying the child is not automatically authorised. If the representative changes, document the new authority and determine whether fresh consent is needed. The guides do not state whether one or two parental signatures are needed (DECISIONS.md D16).
  - **assent dissent**: Information through a person trained or experienced in working with children; assent omitted not because the child is below a sponsor-created band when the child can understand and express a view; an explicit refusal or wish to withdraw of a minor capable of forming an opinion is respected and a representative's signature does not erase dissent. Avoid phrases suggesting that a parent has already decided.
  - **staged assent**: Move to a more mature information document when a child's understanding changes materially, if the approved package provides for staged assent.
  - **reconsent at 18**: SI-PT-main or SI-PT-healthy: express informed consent before continued participation; do not rely indefinitely on the earlier representative consent.
  - **adolescent privacy**: Explain what information parents or representatives will receive and what may stay confidential within the limits of law, safety and the protocol; no promise of absolute confidentiality from parents.
- **adults without capacity**:
  - **always**:
    - SI-PT-legal-rep
    - SI-PT-limited-capacity
  - **note**: Capacity is decision-specific and can change. Legally designated representative with documented authority (Patients' Rights Act, Article 37; where no representative exists the healthcare provider informs the competent authority for appointment). An explicit refusal or resistance of a participant capable of forming an opinion is respected; silence, passivity or inability to sign is not agreement. Participant's own express consent with SI-PT-main when capacity is regained; for fluctuating capacity, document each assessment and avoid burdensome repeated consent. No vague, unlimited future use authorised by the representative.
- **emergency**:
  - **always**:
    - SI-PT-emergency
    - SI-PT-emergency-legal-rep
    - SI-PT-emergency-notice
  - **note**: Article 35 applied directly; no Slovenian national emergency-consent form or broader waiver. Later participant consent even if a representative consented to continuation. Record date and time carefully. Refusal stops further trial-specific intervention and follow-up except necessary and lawful safety actions.
- **impartial witness**:
  - **always**:
    - SI-PT-witness
  - **note**: Witness statement confirming personal observation that the information was accurately explained, the person appeared to understand it, questions were answered, the decision was made freely and the consent expressed matched the record. A representative who cannot write needs the witness section in the representative document (DECISIONS.md D13).
- **reproductive risk**:
  - **always**:
    - SI-PT-reproductive-risk
  - **note**: Applies when reproductive.teratogenic_risk is true: women of childbearing potential and, when relevant, healthy female partners of male participants (KME RS guidance, point 12).
- **pregnancy**:
  - **participant**: SI-PT-pregnancy when follow-up goes beyond the main trial information
  - **partner**: SI-PT-pregnant-partner whenever data are collected from or about the pregnant partner
  - **infant**: SI-PT-infant when follow-up continues after birth
- **healthy volunteers**:
  - **always**:
    - SI-PT-healthy
  - **screening**: SI-PT-screening when invasive procedures or identifiable health-data collection begin before main consent
- **secondary use**:
  - **always**:
    - SI-PT-secondary
  - **note**: Separate choice for each optional purpose (future disease research, optional genetic or biomarker analysis, sharing with academic or commercial collaborators, biobank or reusable database, future contact, record linkage, leftover samples). The KME RS model statement on anonymised scientific use is narrow and is not permission for identifiable, pseudonymised, genetic, commercial or unlimited future use.
- **shareable templates**:
  - SI-PT-pregnant-partner
  - SI-PT-secondary
  - SI-PT-reproductive-risk
- **separate document rules**:
  - Healthy volunteers and patients in one protocol get separate or clearly population-specific documents.
  - Emergency enrolment uses emergency-specific documents, not an ordinary adult ICF.
  - Optional future data or sample use has a separate form or clearly separated optional section with its own choice and signature; refusal must not block main-trial participation.
  - The pregnant partner receives her own document; the enrolled participant cannot consent on her behalf.
  - Genuinely optional activities (future research samples, optional genetic work, contact for other studies, pregnancy-partner follow-up) have separate statements and signature boxes.
  - Re-consent when an approved change affects the basis of participation or legal status changes: reaching 18, regained capacity, a new optional research use or material new safety information.

## Drafting rules

- Participant documents are in Slovenian: natural Slovenian terminology, short sentences, technical terms defined; clear, short, respectful, empathetic and lay-friendly information (KME RS); no unreviewed literal translation; reviewed by a competent native-language clinical reviewer.
- The PIS and ICF may be one integrated document or two coordinated documents; either way the title, protocol identifier, EU trial number, population, version, date and site contact details match, and optional activities are not hidden inside one general agreement.
- Trial identity and purpose: full and plain-language title, sponsor, protocol number, EU trial number, reason for the research, investigational treatment, comparison, why the person is invited; make clear it is research and what differs from ordinary care.
- Procedures, visits and duration: screening, randomisation, blinding, treatment, placebo or comparator, visits, hospital stays, samples, imaging, questionnaires, devices, follow-up and participant responsibilities, with realistic time commitments; identify research-only procedures.
- Risks, burdens and safety: reasonably foreseeable adverse effects, procedural, reproductive and genetic risks, inconvenience, pain, travel, time and uncertainty; safety checks, emergency contacts, discontinuation rules and when the investigator or sponsor may stop participation.
- Teratogenic or reproductive risk: specific written information on pregnancy and fetal risk, contraception and the period during which pregnancy must be avoided, with the KME RS signed acknowledgment (population set reproductive_risk_acknowledgment).
- Benefits and alternatives: no implied personal benefit when none is expected; possible direct benefit, benefit to future patients and uncertainty; reasonable alternatives including standard care; what happens to ordinary healthcare after refusal or withdrawal.
- Voluntariness and withdrawal: refusal or withdrawal without giving a reason and without loss of appropriate healthcare or damage to the relationship with the treating physician; which activities stop, what safety follow-up may still be requested, what happens to samples and why some data must be retained.
- Privacy: controllers and processors, data collected, purposes, recipients, source-record access, coding or pseudonymisation, international transfers, safeguards, retention and rights; state when complete deletion cannot be promised; never present trial consent as the legal basis for all processing (Information Commissioner).
- Biological samples: required samples, purpose, tests, coding, storage, transfers, access, destruction or retention, genetic analysis, incidental findings and withdrawal limits; optional future use as a separate choice.
- Compensation, expenses and insurance: travel reimbursement, meals, accommodation, payment, schedule and what happens after early withdrawal, without improper pressure; care and compensation for trial-related injury with a practical contact route, matching the proof of sponsor and investigator liability cover; no broader promises than the policy supports.
- Results, new information and contacts: whether and how overall results will be available, how new information is communicated, whether individual findings are returned; name, role, address and telephone number of the investigator or qualified contact for questions and urgent medical advice; the trial and participant-rights contacts KME RS expects (national contact points listed on the KME RS page: JAZMP, KME RS, patient-rights representatives, the Human Rights Ombudsman, the Information Commissioner) with current official details, never copied from an old template.
- Consent section: separate statements that the participant received and understood the written and oral information, could ask questions and received satisfactory answers, had enough time, understands voluntariness and the right to withdraw without reason or harm to ordinary care, agrees to the defined trial procedures, understands the injury and insurance information and receives a signed and dated copy; separate statements and signature boxes for genuinely optional activities.
- Signature blocks: participant or legally designated representative and the person conducting the interview, each with printed name, signature and date; witness, adolescent-agreement and representative blocks only in the pathway where they apply; two copies, one for the participant and one for the trial archive (KME RS).
- Blank documents in CTIS with version number and date; no signatures or personal data.
- Avoid coercive, reassuring, promotional or liability-waiving language; explain probabilities in plain language with consistent denominators.
- Participant templates were not supplied; where a reference document or a later template is used, its instructions ([square-bracket] notes, italic notes, '...........' gaps) and the Part II form instructions (yellow [INSERT/IF/OTHERWISE/CHOOSE/NOTE: …] labels) are drafting instructions; none may remain in a final document.
