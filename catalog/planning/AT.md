# National rules – Austria

## Authorities and fee

- **competent authority**: BASG (Federal Office for Safety in Health Care / Bundesamt für Sicherheit im Gesundheitswesen); competent authority, issues the national decision
- **ethics**: The Austrian CTR ethics committees assess Part II and defined Part I ethical aspects through their national platform (AustrianEthics). Sponsors submit and communicate through CTIS; no separate national ethics application.
- **fee**: BASG bills separately from the application assessment: actual proof of payment is not part of a valid submission. Upload a national contact and billing address in the CTIS 'Proof of Payment of Fee' area so the invoice and national decision can be delivered.

## Language rules

- **participant documents**: de
- **administrative documents**:
  - en
- **rule**: For multinational trials the application dossier is submitted in English. The full and public trial titles, the protocol synopsis and the participant-facing materials required by Austria must also be in German. Part II sections K and L participant-facing materials are German; participant-facing insurance information is also subject to the German-language rule. The full German title must match the title on the ICF and insurance materials.
- **non german speakers**: If an Austrian site plans to enrol a participant (or representative) who does not sufficiently understand German, all participant-facing documents for that participant must be available as certified translations into a language they understand. An English master ICF is not a substitute for the German Austria version, and the impartial-witness route is never a substitute for translation.
- **endpoint documents**: Participant-facing endpoint documents (questionnaires, diaries) may belong in Part I under current EU guidance; Austria still applies the participant-language rule to their use. The Austria Part II checklist names no Part II slot for them.
- **form answers**: Part II forms (shared EU templates in content/eu/templates/part-ii) are answered in English, matching the English dossier for multinational trials. The supplied CTIS_AT template set was prepared for German answers; the language for national-only trials is not stated in the guides (DECISIONS.md D1).

## Drafting rules

- Participant documents: the Austrian national ICF template is the recommended structure; another format must cover every topic of the national template (AustrianEthics). The supplied AT participant templates are based on it.
- The insurance and data-protection passages of the Austrian sample ICF are used verbatim, not linguistically rewritten; necessary additions only at the end of the relevant section and without contradiction or repetition. This applies in Draft and in Style.
- Plain German for lay readers; avoid unexplained technical terms and redundant content; explain unavoidable terms at first use; graphics or tables only where they improve understanding. Terms from the Austrian sample text stay unchanged (template instructions).
- Cover at least: nature and purpose; procedures, allocation and duration; risks, inconveniences and burdens; benefits without overstatement; alternatives; right to refuse or withdraw; what happens after stopping; compensation and reimbursement; Austrian no-fault insurance and the insurance contact point; personal-data processing with the mandatory privacy wording; source-record access; samples; pregnancy and contraception where relevant; study, site, emergency, data-protection and patient-advocacy contacts; how and where results become available under the CTR.
- Site contacts are an integral part of the information: full centre name, PI and deputy PI, 24-hour emergency number, site data-protection officer, patient advocacy, and the national contact point for insurance questions. Multicentre: placeholders in the master ICF plus a current site contact list marked 'not for publication' (DECISIONS.md D9).
- Optional services, optional genetic investigations, future research and secondary data use are not buried in the main participation decision: separate yes/no choices or separate documents as population_sets require.
- Withdrawal: after full withdrawal no new study-purpose data are collected through visits, follow-up or re-contact; CTR safety reporting is unaffected. Explain the effect of withdrawal on data already processed (section 41 AMG) with the national wording.
- AustrianEthics Guidance V5: name both insurers in combination studies with different sponsors and insurers; name known recipient countries of pseudonymised data, and for a country without an adequacy decision name it and obtain informed explicit consent; describe ethnic-origin data explicitly when collected; with different reimbursement methods list all options of the Austrian sites and give a choice where a site offers more than one; external reimbursement provider named with its data processing.
- Pregnancy of a participant: state that study medication is stopped, the right to withdraw without reason, which protocol measures continue, whether blinding is broken, which additional data are collected and how; only minimal additional burden and risk (Article 33 CTR).
- Minors: explain the representative's decision and the child's own right to refuse in age-appropriate language; do not merely shorten the adult text; privacy and insurance explained at the child's level without contradicting the legal text in the representative document.
- Consent signatures at the consent visit: wet ink or qualified electronic signature (Austrian national CTR requirements portal); the person conducting the consent interview also signs and dates; the participant or representative receives a copy. The CTIS upload is the blank controlled template.
- Participant templates (JSON): italic text, [square-bracket] notes and "......" gaps are drafting instructions or gaps; follow them and remove them. Part II templates (DOCX): yellow [INSERT/IF/OTHERWISE/CHOOSE/NOTE] labels. Nothing of either may remain in a final document.
