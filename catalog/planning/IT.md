# National rules – Italy

## Authorities and fee

- **competent authority**: AIFA (Agenzia Italiana del Farmaco) acts for Italy in the authorisation and supervision of medicinal-product trials
- **ethics**: The competent Territorial Ethics Committee (CET) performs the ethical review; AIFA-based National Ethics Committees review paediatric trials and advanced therapy medicinal products. The committee is assigned within the national system; its Part II opinion applies nationally. Requests for information are exchanged in CTIS.
- **coordination**: CCNCE (Centro di coordinamento nazionale dei comitati etici territoriali) publishes the national models and guidance: informed-consent guidance and models (27 May 2026), FAQ (10 June 2026), privacy information and consent model (13 August 2026), principal-investigator declaration-of-interests model (v4, 21 April 2024), reimbursement and allowance model, biological-samples model, national clinical-trial agreement v3.
- **fee**: Not covered by the Italy guides (reviewed 23 September 2026). Proof of fee payment uses the CTIS Forms section, not Part II (naming guide). Amount and payment route: see DECISIONS.md, Missing.

## Language rules

- **participant documents**: it
- **administrative documents**:
  - en
  - it
- **rule**: Documents intended for participants must be in Italian for trial conduct and ethics review. Non-participant Part II documents can generally be submitted in English. The insurance certificate must be in Italian. The declaration of interests can be used in the Italian or English version provided by AIFA.
- **emergency exception**: During an officially recognised public-health emergency a narrow arrangement may permit initial English patient documents, but Italian information and consent are still required before enrolment begins. Not a routine submission route.
- **non italian speakers**: Where a potential participant does not understand Italian, arrange qualified language support and translated material appropriate to that person; a cultural mediator is an appropriate safeguard (CCNCE). Document the process. A relative's informal summary is not a substitute.
- **form answers**: Part II forms (shared EU templates in content/eu/templates/part-ii) are answered in Italian, matching the Italian variable sheet supplied with CTIS_IT; English is also accepted for non-participant documents (see DECISIONS.md).

  - **reconsent at 18**: IT-PT-main or IT-PT-healthy, when participants can reach 18 during trial activities or follow-up
  - **under 6**: No age-band template; give an explanation suited to the child's ability even if a separate written assent form is not meaningful, and record how it was given and how the child responded.
  - **band change**: A child who moves into the 12–17 group during the trial receives the adolescent information and assent process.
  - **note**: The CCNCE appendix heading and current model identify the younger group as 6 to 11; check the current source form when a child is close to an age boundary. The template bands (6-11, 12-17) match the authority. The legally operative consent comes from the person(s) entitled under Italian law; the parent model provides signature routes for mother and father or a legal guardian.
- **adults without capacity**:
  - **always**:
    - IT-PT-legal-rep
    - IT-PT-limited-capacity
  - **note**: CCNCE double-consent approach where possible. Representative authority (tutore, curatore, amministratore di sostegno) verified against the court order; a fiduciario is not automatically the legal representative; do not enrol until the valid route is resolved, unless Article 35 applies. Personal consent with IT-PT-main when capacity returns.
- **emergency**:
  - **always**:
    - IT-PT-emergency
  - **note**: Later representative consent uses IT-PT-legal-rep while the participant cannot decide; when the participant becomes capable, their own consent (IT-PT-main or the emergency template's re-consent block).
- **impartial witness**:
  - **always**:
    - IT-PT-witness
  - **note**: Capable participant unable to write only; at least one impartial witness when an alternative recording method is used.
- **pregnancy**:
  - **participant**: IT-PT-pregnancy when follow-up goes beyond the approved main consent and privacy documents
  - **partner**: IT-PT-pregnant-partner whenever the partner's data are collected directly or her records accessed
- **shareable templates**:
  - IT-PT-pregnant-partner
  - IT-PT-secondary
- **separate document rules**:
  - Privacy information and data-processing consent are a separate Italian document, distinct from the clinical-trial consent form (CCNCE FAQ, 10 June 2026; model announced 13 August 2026): document IT-PRIVACY.
  - Collection, storage and future use of samples or data outside the protocol need separate information and consent (IT-PT-secondary); refusal must not prevent main-trial participation unless the activity is scientifically essential and described as such in the protocol.
  - Optional genetic testing, biobanking, future research and optional sub-studies get distinct choices with their own signature or initial fields.
  - A pregnant partner receives a separate document with her own information, privacy and consent; the enrolled participant cannot consent for her.
  - Infant data collected after birth: a separate section or document, with consent of the persons exercising parental responsibility.
  - One national core set: authorised consent documents are nationally valid and are not changed by individual sites (CCNCE FAQ, 10 June 2026); only genuine site variables differ.
  - Re-consent after new safety information, a protocol change, a new optional activity or another material fact: the approved new version, with time for questions and the decision documented.

## Drafting rules

- Participant documents: the CCNCE informed-consent guidance v4 and model forms of 27 May 2026 are the national benchmark. They are structured guidance and facsimiles: adapt them to the trial without deleting required information. Check the CCNCE page immediately before filing (the May 2024 Article 7 evaluation guide is marked under revision).
- Layered structure: one or two pages of key information in plain language, then detailed information, then the consent declarations and signatures.
- Key information states voluntariness, that refusal or withdrawal does not affect ordinary care or legal rights, why the person is invited, the purpose, the investigational nature and main differences from normal care, duration, visits, randomisation, placebo or blinding, main procedures, additional samples, burdens, foreseeable risks, potential benefits (or that none is expected), alternatives, and the most important pregnancy, contraception, privacy, reimbursement, injury and contact information.
- Plain Italian: no medical jargon, unexplained acronyms or English terms in an Italian form; no statements waiving legal rights; no overstated benefit, minimised risk or reimbursement presented as an inducement; avoid the word for payment where it suggests a reward for accepting risk.
- Title block: study title in Italian, lay title where helpful, protocol or EU trial identifier, sponsor or funder, trial site, principal investigator and competent ethics committee.
- Separate standard-care procedures from research-only procedures; give blood volume and frequency, imaging, questionnaires, device use, home activities, travel, confinement and follow-up; explain competitive enrolment where it can close recruitment early.
- Risks: known and reasonably foreseeable, product-class, procedure and reproductive risks and material uncertainties; Phase I and first-in-human trials need particularly direct wording about limited human experience and unknown risks.
- The clinical-consent form explains in plain language that authorised persons may review coded trial data and medical records; detailed GDPR information and data-processing consent go in the separate Italian privacy document. Do not call coded or pseudonymised data anonymous; do not promise deletion of lawfully collected trial data.
- State whether treatment, tests and visits are free; reimbursement of actual trial-related expenses and any permitted documented loss-of-income arrangement, identical to the Part II reimbursement document and the ethics approval.
- Explain trial insurance and the route for reporting suspected injury; never require a waiver of claims; give accessible contacts for study questions, urgent medical concerns, participant rights and data protection, operational at every site.
- Signatures at the consent visit only: participant and the doctor or investigator who informed sign and date, with place and time where the national model includes them; the submission copy is blank and never pre-signed or backdated; a signed copy goes to the participant.
- People who do not understand Italian: qualified language support, translated material and a cultural mediator, with the process documented; a relative's informal summary is not a substitute.
- Electronic consent only case by case with ethics-committee approval, preserving direct interaction, identity verification, access to the information, an auditable signature and delivery of a copy.
- One national core set; only genuine site variables (principal investigator, hospital, contact details, local reimbursement logistics, emergency contacts). Site letterhead is not required; if used, it belongs to the institution conducting or hosting the trial.
- Participant templates (JSON): italic text (red italic in the child template), blue examples, [square-bracket] notes, ALL-CAPITALS instructions (for example INDICARE…, SPECIFICARE…) and ........... gaps are drafting instructions or gaps; follow them and remove them. Underscore lines for the consent visit stay blank. Part II templates (DOCX): yellow [INSERT/IF/OTHERWISE/CHOOSE/NOTE] labels. Nothing of either may remain in a final document.
