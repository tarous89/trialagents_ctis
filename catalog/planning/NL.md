  national_notes: 'CTIS title: K1_Recruitment arrangements. Signed at CTIS submission: no

    First act of recruitment, identification and approach of potential participants, access to identifiable records, consent setting, reflection time and the special procedures for minors, adults lacking capacity, witnesses (section 5) and emergencies (section 6). The protocol must describe every consent pathway used.

    National additions to the form: - **v105**: Required in the Netherlands: in Dutch, state that every trial-specific advertisement or recruitment item addressed directly to potential participants is submitted under K2 and that only the Dutch version is assessed; general institutional information that is not study-specific is not recruitment material. Recruitment items are blank at submission and not signed. Where the trial combines the medicinal product with a medical device or IVD, refer to the coordinated Dutch process described in the cover letter.

    - **v108**: Required in the Netherlands: in Dutch, state that only the Dutch information sheet and consent form are assessed (IVO model v1.1, 14 August 2026, for capable participants aged 16 or older; mandatory from 1 January 2027, the previous PIF model permitted until then); that capable participants aged 16 or older consent themselves; that the participant (or representative) and the researcher or authorised interviewer sign and date the form at the consent visit and the participant receives the complete information sheet, cover page and a signed copy; that reflection time fits the trial (CCMO presents at least 48 hours as a benchmark, not a fixed rule); that explicit consent is asked for access to medical records or information from a general practitioner, specialist or pharmacist; that optional items have separate Yes/No choices; and, where non-Dutch-speaking participants are planned, that written and oral information is given in a language they understand, the sponsor is responsible for an accurate translation aligned with the authorised Dutch version, a translation certificate follows with the first Part II substantial modification, and the language of the oral discussion is documented in the medical record.

    - **v110**: Required in the Netherlands when adults lacking capacity are included: in Dutch, describe the protective ''no, unless'' conditions (direct benefit possible, or non-therapeutic research that can only be done with this group at minimal risk and burden compared with standard treatment or the nature and severity of the condition); how capacity is assessed for this decision, by whom, and reassessed if it fluctuates; the Dutch representative order (court-appointed curator or mentor; person authorised in writing by the participant; spouse, registered partner or other life partner; parent; adult child; adult sibling) and how identity, role and authority are confirmed and recorded; that the participant receives information adapted to their capacity and that a verbal or behavioural objection is respected and stops study procedures (apart from an immediate safety step); that the participant''s own consent is obtained without undue delay when capacity is gained or regained; and that temporary incapacity alone does not permit enrolment without prior consent outside an authorised Article 35 trial.

    - **v114**: Required in the Netherlands when minors are included: in Dutch, state that persons under 16 are minors for trial consent under the protective ''no, unless'' approach; for a child under 12 both parents with parental authority, or the guardian, consent and the child receives age-appropriate information (CCMO discussion sheet); for a child aged 12 through 15 the child also gives written consent on their own form in addition to both parents or the guardian; how the adults with parental authority are identified (separated parents, remote signatures, sole guardian); that the parent consent declaration states that consent for continued participation no longer applies if the minor resists, in words or behaviour, and how investigators recognise, document and respond to resistance; that a child enrolled before 12 gives explicit own consent before continuing after turning 12; that no new consent action is needed solely because the participant turns 16 (CCMO); and that participants aged 16 or older follow the adult pathway.

    - **v117**: Required in the Netherlands when the witness route is planned: in Dutch, state that the route applies only to a capable participant or representative who understands and decides but cannot write, sign or read the written material (Article 29 CTR), never for lack of capacity (legal representative) or a missing translation; how the witness is selected (independent of the trial, not the investigator, delegated study staff, sponsor staff or anyone with a financial or professional interest; family members only where independence is assured), that the witness is present throughout, signs and dates an attestation and does not decide for the person; which approved alternative method records consent; and that an interpreter and a witness have different roles.

    - **v121**: Required in the Netherlands when emergency enrolment is used: in Dutch, address every Article 35 condition and the CCMO memorandum on deferred consent (7 April 2020); the medically justified therapeutic window; the prior-objection check; that consent is sought from the participant or, while the participant remains unable, from the legally designated representative in the Dutch order without undue delay (CCMO does not endorse a routine fixed delay such as 72 hours); the participant''s own consent when capacity returns; that refusal stops new trial procedures and data collection but not ordinary care; that data lawfully gathered under Article 35 remain in the trial (EU CTR Q&A, March 2026); how a death before consent is handled (data may be used if the person had not objected while alive; the representative or next of kin is informed); and, for a mixed population, the compelling justification for enrolling some participants without prior consent.

    Completed by: assistant

# National rules – Netherlands

## Authorities and fee

- **competent authority**: CCMO's National Clinical Trial Office validates the Dutch application, considers the sponsor's proposed MREC and assigns the reviewing committee (it may assign another for competence, timing or expertise). The guides describe no separate competent-authority assessment.
- **ethics**: One accredited Medical Research Ethics Committee (MREC), or CCMO for specified trial types (including medicinal-product research involving pregnant or breastfeeding participants), assesses Part I and Part II in one integrated review.
- **fee**: Invoiced after submission: upload the Dutch invoice-details template under Proof of payment of fee; no advance proof of payment; CCMO sends an online invoice payable within 30 days.

## Language rules

- **participant documents**: nl
- **administrative documents**:
  - nl
  - en
- **rule**: Participant information and consent forms, their appendices, other material forming part of consent and recruitment material are submitted in Dutch; only the Dutch versions are assessed. The guides require Dutch only for participant-facing material: the official Dutch R1 form is available in English, and the VGO is available in Dutch and English at the institution's choice. They state no language for the other administrative documents.
- **non dutch speakers**: A potential participant who does not understand Dutch well enough receives written and oral information in a language they understand; the sponsor is responsible for the translation and for keeping its content and tone aligned with the authorised Dutch document. When inclusion of non-Dutch-speaking participants is planned, a translation certificate is submitted with the first Part II substantial modification (not needed for an unexpected individual case, an English version when the Dutch document is a direct translation of it, later changes, recruitment material or other documents shown to participants). Document the language of the oral discussion in the medical record.
- **endpoint documents**: Questionnaires or diaries recording trial endpoints are described in the protocol and may be submitted under Part I D4; they are not automatically Part II documents.
- **form answers**: Shared EU forms (content/eu/templates/part-ii) are answered in Dutch, as the CTIS_NL template set and variable sheet target Dutch answers. English answers only if the user decides so (DECISIONS.md D1).

## Populations, age bands and representatives

- **authority**: CCMO (Central Committee on Research Involving Human Subjects) guidance on participant information, consent, research with minors and incapacitated participants, deferred consent in emergency research (memorandum 7 April 2020) and future research; IVO model version 1.1 (14 August 2026); CCMO minor templates (14 August 2025); Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 Articles 28 to 35; EU CTR Q&A (March 2026). As recorded in the Netherlands guides reviewed 23 September 2026.
- **adults**:
  - **age of own consent**: 16
  - **note**: Capable participants aged 16 or older consent for themselves on the adult IVO pathway (NL-PT-main or NL-PT-healthy). Parent signatures are no longer required solely because the person is under 18.
- **minors**:
  - **definition**: under 16
  - **age bands**:
    - **band**: under 12
    - **template**: NL-PT-age-6-11
    - **reader**: child
    - **note**: Discussion sheet only; the child gives no legal consent. Both parents with parental authority, or the guardian, sign NL-PT-parents.
    - **band**: 12-15
    - **template**: NL-PT-age-12-17
    - **reader**: adolescent
    - **note**: The child signs their own Dutch information and consent form, and both parents with parental authority, or the guardian, also sign NL-PT-parents.
  - **always**:
    - NL-PT-parents
  - **reconsent at 12**: Mandatory: a child enrolled before 12 gives explicit own consent (NL-PT-age-12-17) before further trial procedures after turning 12; the parents' consent stands.
  - **reconsent at 16**: No new consent action solely because the participant turns 16 (CCMO); new consent may be needed for another reason such as important new information.
  - **participants 16 17**: Adult pathway (NL-PT-main or NL-PT-healthy); if lacking capacity, the legal-representative pathway, not the minor pathway.
  - **resistance**: Mandatory: the parent consent declaration states that if the minor resists participation, consent for continued participation no longer applies. Resistance can be in words or behaviour; silence is not assent (CCMO code of conduct on objection by minors, 2002).
  - **representatives**: Identify which adults have parental authority before consent; a parent attending the visit cannot sign for the other parent; address separated parents, remote signatures and a sole guardian before recruitment.
  - **conflicts**:
    - Template bands 6-11 and 12-17 (file names and descriptions) versus CCMO bands under 12 and 12-15: the authority's bands are used; the supplied file names are kept.
    - Parents template description 'younger than 18' versus CCMO under 16: under 16 is used (the template's content instructions already say under 16).
- **adults without capacity**:
  - **always**:
    - NL-PT-legal-rep
    - NL-PT-limited-capacity
  - **representative order**:
    - court-appointed curator or mentor
    - person authorised in writing by the participant
    - spouse, registered partner or other life partner
    - parent
    - adult child who can reasonably be contacted
    - adult sibling who can reasonably be contacted
  - **note**: 'No, unless' conditions; capacity is decision-specific and assessed, not inferred from diagnosis. Information adapted to capacity is mandatory (NL-PT-limited-capacity, as short and supported as needed). Objection, verbal or behavioural, is respected. Own consent (NL-PT-main) without undue delay when capacity is gained or regained. Temporary incapacity alone does not permit enrolment without prior consent outside an authorised Article 35 trial.
- **emergency**:
  - **always**:
    - NL-PT-emergency
    - NL-PT-legal-rep
  - **note**: NL-PT-legal-rep adapted to the deferred-consent context for the representative while the participant remains unable to consent (DECISIONS.md D12); NL-PT-emergency for the participant once capacity returns; the minors' set when the participant is a minor. Mixed populations only with compelling justification in the protocol (EU CTR Q&A March 2026, which is newer than the CCMO memorandum of 2020, allows it when the reasons are clear).
- **impartial witness**:
  - **always**:
    - NL-PT-witness
  - **note**: Only when a capable participant or representative cannot write, sign or read the written material; never for lack of capacity or a missing translation.
- **pregnancy**:
  - **participant**: Separate NL-PT-pregnancy document only when the authorised IVO does not clearly cover the planned follow-up, extra record access, infant information or duration. Trials enrolling pregnant or breastfeeding participants are reviewed by CCMO and address pregnancy in the main documents.
  - **partner**: NL-PT-pregnant-partner before any direct follow-up of a pregnant partner; the partner signs their own form.
  - **infant**: Infant data only under the approved pathway with consent of the holders of parental authority.
- **shareable templates**:
  - NL-PT-pregnant-partner
  - NL-PT-secondary
- **separate document rules**:
  - Optional future use of data or remaining bodily material: separate Yes/No choices for data and for bodily material, never one blanket consent; a separate secondary-use ICF (NL-PT-secondary) is allowed when it improves clarity and stays linked to the main L1 (DECISIONS.md D11).
  - A pregnant partner receives a separate document addressed to them; the enrolled participant cannot consent for the partner.
  - Adults lacking capacity: the participant's adapted information does not replace the representative document; both are submitted.
  - Children under 12: the discussion sheet supports the conversation and does not replace the parents' or guardian's consent form.
  - Healthy volunteers and patients in one trial: separate information where risks, benefit, procedures, restrictions, compensation or alternatives differ.

## Drafting rules

- Participant documents are Dutch; only the Dutch versions are assessed. The English IRP is a drafting aid only and cannot be submitted; follow the Dutch IVO model text and instructions even when an English master is used upstream.
- Adults aged 16 or older: CCMO IVO model version 1.1 (14 August 2026), strongly recommended now and mandatory from 1 January 2027; the PIF model may be used until then only as an intentional choice. An already authorised PIF need not be converted.
- IVO model rules: B1 language level; cover page with the main decision points at most two pages; complete IVO including consent form and appendices at most 4,000 words (more only with a reason in the cover letter); accessible sans-serif font listed in the model at no less than 12 pt or 16 px; retain standard text unless the model permits editing or deletion; delete instructions, unused options and irrelevant sections; keep the questions, tick boxes and completion fields that belong to the consent discussion; preserve the general CCMO information references.
- Layered risk information: the main decision-changing risks on the cover page (for healthy volunteers the three main risks), symptoms that require contacting the study team in the main text, the fuller side-effect and procedure-risk list in an appendix. Do not copy technical risk language or eligibility criteria from the protocol.
- Separate research procedures from usual care; a short schedule may clarify repeated visits. Make the worst realistic time commitment visible before consent (healthy volunteers).
- State whether direct benefit is possible, uncertain or absent; never imply therapeutic benefit for a healthy volunteer; payment is not a benefit and is kept in its own section.
- Privacy: name the data and special categories used and why, coding, who sees identifiable data (by role), storage location and fixed retention period, controller(s), data-protection officer and complaint contact (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens), transfers outside the EU and safeguards, the Health and Youth Care Inspectorate access statement; never call coded data anonymous. Ask explicitly for access to medical records or information from a GP, specialist or pharmacist. Match the Dutch R1 form and the GDPR statement.
- Insurance: state whether Dutch WMO participant insurance applies or an exemption was granted; include the insurance appendix unless exempt; match insurer, policy, claims contact and coverage to O1. A disclosed expected risk is not automatically compensated.
- Compensation, travel reimbursement and possible tax treatment match the Dutch P1 form; not presented as a benefit; for healthy volunteers give the gross amount or calculation, schedule and partial-payment conditions.
- Optional items each get a separate Yes/No choice (future use of data, future use of remaining bodily material, future contact, disclosure of allocation, DNA research outside the core question, AI analysis outside the core question, data to an external travel provider); refusal never blocks participation or care; no blanket consent.
- Include an independent-expert contact only when it is part of the authorised process (no longer automatically required under the CTR).
- Minors: the parent consent declaration must contain the statement that if the minor resists, consent for continued participation no longer applies; add it where the template's wording is weaker and record the addition as a finding. Child documents use their own reading level, not a shrunken adult text.
- Re-consent triggers are operational: new safety information, a child reaching 12, recovery of capacity; give the participant the complete information sheet and a signed copy.
- Translations: where non-Dutch speakers are planned, written and oral information in a language they understand; sponsor responsible for accuracy and tone matching the authorised Dutch version; translation certificate with the first Part II substantial modification (not for an unexpected individual case, a direct translation of the English master, later changes, recruitment material or other documents shown to participants). An interpreter or witness does not replace understandable written information.
- Have trial-specific wording checked by a lay reader from the intended population where possible.
- Participant templates (JSON): italic text, <angle-bracket> and [square-bracket] notes, 'Vul in'/'Kies' labels and '......' gaps are drafting instructions or gaps; follow them and remove them. Part II templates (DOCX): yellow [INSERT/IF/OTHERWISE/CHOOSE/NOTE] labels. Nothing of either may remain in a final document.
