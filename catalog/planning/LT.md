    - **v110**: Required in Lithuania when incapacitated adults are included: in Lithuanian, state which categories of person entitled under Lithuanian law (Law on Ethics of Biomedical Research: spouse, specified close relatives, appointed representative, guardian of a person adjudged incapacitated in healthcare matters, curator together with the person where legal capacity is limited in healthcare matters) may consent and how the site verifies and documents the representative''s authority and legal basis (not a generic label such as ''family member''); how capacity is assessed for the actual decision (age, serious illness or diagnosis alone do not establish incapacity) and who reassesses it; how the participant receives adapted information and is involved as far as possible; how dissent, including non-verbal objection where clinically relevant, is identified, recorded and respected; that only compensation for expenses and loss of earnings is offered; and who assesses regained capacity and which current ICF version is then used for the participant''s own consent.

    - **v114**: Required in Lithuania when minors are included: in Lithuanian, describe the two national age groups: for children under 12, how the child is informed orally in a way appropriate to age and maturity, whether written information is also provided, and that the information process and the child''s opinion are documented in the trial records; for children aged 12 and above, that information is given orally and in writing and that the child signs an assent form when capable of understanding it. State that consent of both parents is normally required, with the defined exceptions (a parent deceased, incapacitated, declared dead or missing by a court, or with restricted parental rights; a notarised power of attorney for health-related decisions; a court grant of exclusive decision rights), that the reason for a single parental signature is documented, and that physical absence or inconvenience alone is not enough. State that a capable child''s wish not to participate or to withdraw is respected even when the parents consent, except where non-participation or withdrawal would be contrary to the child''s interests, when the representatives decide taking the investigator''s opinion into account; and describe re-consent with the current approved adult ICF when the participant reaches the age of legal competence.

    - **v117**: Required in Lithuania when an impartial witness may be needed: in Lithuanian, state why the witnessed pathway may be needed (participant or legally designated representative can decide but cannot write), which participants may use it, how the impartial witness is selected so that impartiality is preserved (not left to ad hoc site practice; no person whose interests could compromise the record), how the information is communicated, which alternative method records the participant''s consent, that the witness and the person conducting the interview sign and date the consent document, and how the participant receives a copy. A witness does not replace a legally designated representative and does not compensate for a participant not understanding the language.

    - **v121**: Required in Lithuania when emergency enrolment is used: in Lithuanian, explain why prior consent is impossible in the defined emergency, how the urgency is recognised, how the situation and eligibility for emergency enrolment are documented, how the investigator checks for a known prior objection, why a representative cannot be reached within the therapeutic window, what information is given as soon as possible after the intervention, who seeks consent to continue (without undue delay; for minors in line with the Lithuanian parent and child rules), and what happens if the participant or representative refuses later consent, including the right to object to the use of data already obtained.

# National rules – Lithuania

## Authorities and fee

- **competent authority**: VVKT (Valstybinė vaistų kontrolės tarnyba prie Lietuvos Respublikos sveikatos apsaugos ministerijos, State Medicines Control Agency under the Ministry of Health): carries out the scientific assessment and issues the national authorisation.
- **ethics**: Lithuanian Bioethics Committee (Lietuvos bioetikos komitetas): provides the ethical review conclusion used for the national decision; publishes the national Part II document list, recommendations and sample forms (approved 17 May 2022, amended 18 October 2022) and an FAQ. Its sample forms are recommended, not mandatory; an equivalent format is acceptable if it fully covers the required information.
- **fee**: A fixed Lithuanian state fee applies to authorisation of a clinical trial and to a substantial modification; proof of payment is a mandatory item of the national list (LT-FEE, CTIS Forms section). Amount and payment route are not stated in the guides (DECISIONS.md, Missing).
- **national guidance**: Lithuanian Bioethics Committee Part II recommendations and sample forms (17 May 2022, amended 18 October 2022), ICF recommendations and FAQ; Law on Ethics of Biomedical Research; EudraLex Volume 10 including CTR Q&A version 7.3 (July 2026); Regulation (EU) No 536/2014.

## Language rules

- **participant documents**: lt
- **administrative documents**:
  - lt
- **rule**: The Lithuanian Bioethics Committee states that the Part II documents covered by its national recommendations must be submitted in Lithuanian: recruitment and informed consent procedure, participant information, investigator suitability, declarations of interest, site suitability, insurance, financial arrangements, proof of payment, GDPR compliance and biological samples. English source documents may be used for global drafting, but the Lithuania package is built around the Lithuanian versions.
- **non lithuanian speakers**: If a person who does not understand Lithuanian may be consented, define the language and consent pathway in advance, give the information in a language the person understands, and describe the handling in K1. A witness does not compensate for a participant not understanding the language.
- **form answers**: Shared EU forms (content/eu/templates/part-ii) are answered in Lithuanian, matching the supplied Lithuanian variable sheet.
- **third party documents**: Insurer certificates, training certificates and other documents issued by others are also covered by the Lithuanian-language rule; see DECISIONS.md D2.

    - **rule**: Oral information appropriate to age and maturity (mandatory); state whether written information is also provided; written child information only when developmentally appropriate (not a universal mandatory written format); the information process and the child's opinion are documented in the trial records; no assent signature required by the national rule.
    - **band**: 12 and above (up to the age of legal competence)
    - **template**: LT-PT-assent-12-plus
    - **template status**: missing
    - **reader**: adolescent
    - **rule**: Information orally and in writing (mandatory); the child signs an assent form when capable of understanding (conditional); language suited to age and maturity, not the adult ICF.
  - **age band basis**: Lithuanian national Part II recommendations distinguish children under 12 from those aged 12 and above. The guides do not state the age of legal competence as a number (DECISIONS.md D12).
  - **always**:
    - LT-PT-parents
  - **representatives**: Consent from both parents is normally required. Exceptions: one parent deceased, incapacitated, declared dead or missing by a court, or with restricted parental rights; a notarised power of attorney authorising one parent to represent the other in health-related decisions; a court grant of the exclusive right to one parent. The site documents why only one parent signs; physical absence or inconvenience alone does not remove the second-parent requirement.
  - **assent dissent**: Assent is not phrased as the child's legal consent. A child capable of understanding may refuse or withdraw, and the wish is respected even when the parents consent, except where non-participation or withdrawal would be contrary to the child's interests; then the representatives decide, taking the investigator's opinion into account. Documents and site process explain how dissent is recognised, documented and escalated.
  - **reconsent at legal competence**: LT-PT-main or LT-PT-healthy (the current approved adult ICF, not the historic version) before continued participation.
  - **payment**: No incentives beyond compensation for expenses and loss of earnings directly related to participation (Article 32 CTR, as for incapacitated adults).
- **adults without capacity**:
  - **always**:
    - LT-PT-legal-rep
  - **conditional**:
    - LT-PT-limited-capacity
  - **note**: Only when the person's health state prevents a reasonable assessment of their interests (or a formal legal status gives another person a role in consent); not because of age, serious illness or a diagnosis alone. The entitled person under the Law on Ethics of Biomedical Research (spouse, specified close relatives, appointed representative, guardian, curator together with the person) depends on the participant's factual and legal status; the legal basis is recorded. Adapted information where the participant can engage; dissent respected; re-consent with LT-PT-main when capacity is regained.
- **emergency**:
  - **always**:
    - LT-PT-emergency
  - **conditional**:
    - LT-PT-legal-rep
  - **note**: Representative version where the population may remain incapable. For minors use the minors rules (LT-PT-parents and the age-band documents) for later consent. No national emergency ICF template; do not invent a waiver form.
- **impartial witness**:
  - **always**:
    - LT-PT-witness
  - **note**: Inability to write, not lack of capacity or language. A representative who cannot write also uses this pathway (witness block in the representative document).
- **pregnancy**:
  - **participant**: LT-PT-pregnancy only when follow-up involves data or record access not covered by the main ICF
  - **partner**: LT-PT-pregnant-partner whenever partner or infant data are collected from the partner or her records
- **secondary use**:
  - **always**:
    - LT-PT-secondary
  - **note**: Or a distinct opt-in choice in the main ICF (DECISIONS.md D15). Parent or representative versions when minors or incapacitated adults are included.
- **shareable templates**:
  - LT-PT-pregnant-partner
  - LT-PT-secondary
- **separate document rules**:
  - Use a separate or adapted document set whenever a population has different consent logic (Lithuania patient information and consent guide).
  - Minors: parent consent document plus age-appropriate child information and assent; never an adult ICF with a parent signature line added.
  - Healthy volunteers: a healthy-volunteer version, not a renamed patient ICF.
  - Future use of samples or coded data: a separate future-use ICF or a distinct opt-in choice; refusal does not affect main-trial participation (Lithuanian Bioethics Committee).
  - A pregnant partner receives a separate document addressed to her; the enrolled participant cannot consent on her behalf; participant and partner pathways are not mixed.
  - Emergency enrolment: later participant and representative documents describe the emergency situation; standard withdrawal text is not reused if it does not fit.
  - Re-consent at the age of legal competence or after regained capacity uses the current approved adult ICF.

## Drafting rules

- Participant documents are in Lithuanian, written understandably, avoiding specialist terminology where possible; translate the meaning of the approved master content, not word for word; medical terms understandable to the target population; consistent terms throughout.
- Build each document from the actual protocol and submitted Part II information; generic consent language is not enough if it does not match what will happen at Lithuanian sites.
- Make clear that this is research; do not imply that the investigational treatment is proven to help; possible benefits without overstating; alternatives where relevant.
- Core content: sponsor and conductor, purpose, why the person is invited, design (randomisation, placebo), procedures, visits, tests and treatments, duration and follow-up, foreseeable risks, burdens and inconveniences, pregnancy and contraception precautions, samples, confidentiality and data processing, reimbursement, compensation and insurance, right to refuse or withdraw, what happens to data already collected after withdrawal, contacts for the trial, participant rights and research-related injury.
- Short sections, plain explanations; separate what is required for the main trial from optional choices; the reader can tell which procedures are research-specific, what is optional and what happens if they say no.
- State that the investigational medicinal product and trial health checks are free of charge where that is the case (Lithuanian Bioethics Committee recommendation).
- Reimbursement of travel and other costs and any compensation for time explained; an approximate hourly amount stated when time is compensated; contraception costs compensated when contraception is required specifically because of the trial; amounts identical to P1 and P2.
- Insurance or indemnification arrangements and how to seek compensation for trial-related harm, consistent with the submitted certificate or policy.
- Privacy in participant language: confidentiality, the data controller where relevant, data sources and categories, who may access identifiable or coded data, recipients, storage and the participant's rights; consistent with the GDPR statement and actual data flows; trial consent is not a catch-all permission for every future data use. Race or ethnicity data only when scientifically justified, and the justification explained.
- Samples: why collected, analyses for the main trial, where sent or stored and how long retained; future use beyond the main trial is a separate, optional, not pre-selected choice.
- Home visits are exceptional and are described in the protocol and consent documents, especially where optional.
- Electronic consent only with an electronic signature method whose legal validity is established; a simple electronic signature is not automatically equivalent to a handwritten one.
- Signature blocks: participant or consent-giver and the investigator or authorised research person, with names, signatures, date and time, and the basis of representation where another person consents; the participant's copy is also signed by the investigator or authorised research person; witness blocks only where the pathway may be used; blank and unsigned in CTIS.
- Filenames and document titles distinguish population and use case; ICF version number and date match the document register.
- Participant templates were not supplied; where a reference document or a later template is used, its instructions ([square-bracket] notes, italic notes, '...........' gaps) and the Part II form instructions (yellow [INSERT/IF/OTHERWISE/CHOOSE/NOTE: ...] labels) are drafting instructions; none may remain in a final document.
