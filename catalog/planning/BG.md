    Inclusion procedures with the first recruitment action clearly identified; if advertisements are used, a general description of how responses will be handled and how unsuitable respondents will receive information or advice. Version and date on the document. The consent-procedure sections of template v2.0 are completed for every consent pathway in the plan (incapacitated adults, minors, impartial witness, emergency) with the Bulgarian rules in national_fields.

    National additions to the form: - **v105**: Bulgaria: recruitment arrangements and every recruitment material are in Bulgarian (BDA); each recruitment document carries its version and date.

    - **v108**: Bulgaria: participant information and ICF in Bulgarian, with the corresponding Bulgaria version in English (BDA). Under Ordinance No 31 on Good Clinical Practice the oral and written information avoids medical jargon and is understandable for the participant, legal representative and, where needed, independent witness; enough time and opportunity to discuss and decide; the participant or legal representative and the person conducting the consent discussion sign and personally date the written consent before enrolment; the participant or representative receives a copy of the signed and dated form and the written information.

    - **v110**: Bulgaria: under Article 96 of the Medicinal Products in Human Medicine Act consent for an incapacitated adult is given by the legal representative; in the situation of Article 162(3) of the Health Act by the person appointed by the court. The representative''s consent represents the participant''s presumed will and may be withdrawn without negative consequences. The participant is informed at a level consistent with their ability to understand and personally signs and dates the written consent if able (Ordinance No 31); an expressed wish to refuse or withdraw is taken into account by the investigator and, when necessary, the principal investigator. The participant''s own consent is obtained if capacity returns. Legal incapacity is not inferred from a diagnosis alone.

    - **v114**: Bulgaria (Article 97 of the Medicinal Products in Human Medicine Act): under 14, written informed consent from both parents or the guardians; 14 to 17, written informed consent from the minor and both parents, or from the custodian where applicable, and the ICF for this age group contains a signature field for the minor (BDA). If one parent is unknown, deceased, deprived of parental rights or does not hold parental rights after divorce, consent is given by the minor (14 to 17) and the parent who exercises parental rights. Information on the trial, risks and benefits is given in a form the minor understands by a physician experienced with children and adolescents. The minor''s explicit refusal or wish to withdraw is taken into account; parental or custodian consent may be withdrawn without negative consequences. The participant''s own express consent is obtained on reaching 18.

    - **v117**: Bulgaria: Ordinance No 31 requires an independent witness when the participant or the legal representative cannot read; the witness does not participate in and has no interest in the trial, cannot be influenced by the trial participants, is present during the whole consent discussion, and signs and personally dates the ICF after the oral confirmation of the decision. Where the participant cannot write, consent is given and recorded by appropriate alternative means in the presence of at least one impartial witness (Regulation (EU) No 536/2014). Describe the intended alternative means. The witness is not a legal representative and the interviewer still signs.

    - **v121**: Bulgaria: in addition to Article 35, the Bulgarian minor (Article 97) and adult representative (Article 96) rules apply to the consent sought after the intervention; consent to continue is sought without undue delay; the participant''s own consent is obtained when capacity returns; the person is informed of the right to object to the use of data obtained; the later consent documents are in Bulgarian with the Bulgaria English version (no English-only exception); the record distinguishes the Article 35 enrolment decision from the later informed-consent event (no retrospective consent date).

# National rules – Bulgaria

## Authorities and fee

- **competent authority**: Bulgarian Drug Agency (BDA, Изпълнителна агенция по лекарствата): national competent authority; publishes the national list of Part I and Part II documents and language expectations (10 July 2024) and the Bulgaria site-suitability declaration template
- **ethics**: Ethics Committee for Clinical Trials (EKKI, Комисия по етика за клинични изпитвания), established under the Minister of Health, performs the national ethics assessment and publishes its CTIS procedures
- **submission**: Initial applications under Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 are submitted through CTIS, not through the BDA registry.
- **fee**: Not covered by the Bulgaria guides (reviewed 23 September 2026). Proof of fee payment uses the CTIS Forms section, not Part II (naming guide). See DECISIONS.md, Missing.

## Populations, age bands and representatives

- **authority**: Bulgarian Drug Agency (BDA), Documents submitted under Annex I and Annex II of Regulation (EU) No 536/2014, 10 July 2024; Medicinal Products in Human Medicine Act, Articles 91, 96 and 97; Ordinance No 31 on Good Clinical Practice (sections 4.8.6 to 4.8.12 and the definition of independent witness); Health Act, Article 162(3); Regulation (EU) No 536/2014, Articles 28 to 35 (as recorded in the Bulgaria guides, reviewed 23 September 2026)
- **minors**:
  - **legal age groups**:
    - **group**: under 14
    - **consent**: written informed consent of both parents or of the guardians; represents the child's presumed will and can be withdrawn
    - **child role**: age-appropriate information from a physician experienced with children and adolescents; explicit refusal or wish to withdraw taken into account
    - **group**: 14-17
    - **consent**: written informed consent of the minor and of both parents, or of the custodian where applicable
    - **child role**: own written informed consent; the ICF contains a signature field for the minor (BDA), not only an assent line
  - **age bands**:
    - **band**: 6-11
    - **legal group**: under 14
    - **template**: BG-PT-age-6-11
    - **reader**: child
    - **band**: 12-13
    - **legal group**: under 14
    - **template**: BG-PT-age-12-13
    - **reader**: child (minor under 14)
    - **band**: 14-17
    - **legal group**: 14-17
    - **template**: BG-PT-age-14-17
    - **reader**: adolescent
  - **always**:
    - BG-PT-parents
  - **single parent exception**: Written consent by the minor (14–17) and the parent who exercises parental rights only when the other parent is unknown, deceased, deprived of parental rights or does not hold parental rights after divorce; the CTIS document makes this route identifiable.
  - **band change**: A participant who reaches 14 during the trial gives their own written consent on BG-PT-age-14-17 before continuing (template instruction, consistent with Article 97).
  - **reconsent at 18**: BG-PT-main or BG-PT-healthy: the participant's express consent before continuing after reaching 18, reflecting the current trial stage.
  - **under 6**: No age-band template. The child still receives information in a form the child can understand (Article 97); whether to prepare a separate document is decided per study (DECISIONS.md D9).
  - **note**: The template bands 6–11 and 12–13 subdivide the authority's under-14 group and 14–17 matches it, so there is no band conflict. Do not create one generic minor form that hides which signatures apply to which age group.
- **adults without capacity**:
  - **always**:
    - BG-PT-legal-rep
    - BG-PT-limited-capacity
  - **representative**: Legal representative (Article 96); in the situation of Article 162(3) of the Health Act the person appointed by the court. Do not invent a broader category of proxy decision-maker.
  - **reconsent on capacity**: BG-PT-main when the participant becomes capable of legally valid consent.
  - **note**: Legal incapacity is not inferred from dementia, psychiatric illness, acute disease or communication difficulty alone. The participant's objection is taken into account by the investigator and, when necessary, the principal investigator.
- **emergency**:
  - **always**:
    - BG-PT-emergency
  - **note**: Later representative consent uses BG-PT-legal-rep while the adult cannot decide; minors also need BG-PT-parents and the age-band document. The participant's own consent with BG-PT-main once capable.
- **impartial witness**:
  - **always**:
    - BG-PT-witness
  - **triggers**: Participant or legal representative cannot read (Ordinance No 31); participant cannot write (Regulation 536/2014).
  - **note**: BG-PT-witness is written for a capable adult participant. When a legal representative, parent or minor cannot read, the witness block goes into that pathway's document (DECISIONS.md D5).
- **pregnancy**:
  - **participant**: BG-PT-pregnancy only when follow-up goes beyond the main consent
  - **partner**: BG-PT-pregnant-partner whenever identifiable data are collected from the partner or her records are accessed
- **shareable templates**:
  - BG-PT-pregnant-partner
  - BG-PT-secondary
- **separate document rules**:
  - Every participant document is prepared in Bulgarian with the corresponding Bulgaria version in English, aligned in content, version and date (BDA).
  - The standard adult PIS and ICF is not stretched to cover every population with one signature page; each consent pathway has its own clear document or signature structure.
  - Optional future use of data or samples outside the protocol is a separate optional choice or document (BG-PT-secondary) whose refusal does not affect main-study participation.
  - A pregnant partner receives a separate document; the enrolled participant cannot sign for her.
  - Identifiable infant data after birth need a separate post-natal consent of the persons with parental rights (pregnancy templates' instruction).
  - Re-consent after a material change: assess whether updated Part II documents and re-consent are needed before implementing the change.

## Drafting rules

- Participant documents: the BDA publishes no mandatory national PIS or ICF text. Use the supplied Bulgarian template for each population, Regulation (EU) No 536/2014, the Medicinal Products in Human Medicine Act (Articles 96 and 97), Ordinance No 31 on Good Clinical Practice and the current EU Part II procedure template v2.0.
- Every participant document is in Bulgarian, with the corresponding Bulgaria version in English; both identify the same content version and date. A technically correct translation is not enough: the Bulgarian text must read as a participant document.
- Plain Bulgarian without medical jargon (Ordinance No 31): short sentences, necessary clinical terms defined at first use, practical consequences explained (for example what randomisation means for the participant), headings that help the reader find risks, visits, alternatives, withdrawal, contacts and compensation; no dense blocks copied from the protocol.
- Cover, as the protocol requires: research nature and purpose; investigational treatment and chance of assignment to each treatment; procedures including invasive ones; participant responsibilities and experimental aspects; reasonably foreseeable risks and inconveniences; risks to an embryo, fetus or nursing infant; expected benefits or a clear statement that no direct clinical benefit is expected; alternatives with their important risks and benefits; compensation or treatment for trial-related injury; payment or reimbursement; expected costs; voluntariness and the right to refuse or withdraw; access to source medical records for monitoring, audit, ethics and regulators with confidentiality protections; protection of identity; new information; contacts for trial information, participant rights and injury; foreseeable reasons participation may end; expected duration; approximate number of participants.
- Insurance, compensation, contacts and data-processing wording agrees with O1, P1/P2, R1 and S1. Do not insert policy details that may change unless the participant needs them.
- Data protection: explain practical processing, confidentiality, source-record access, recipients and participant rights consistently with R1; do not present trial-participation consent as the only GDPR legal basis; distinguish stopping participation from handling of data already lawfully collected; never promise deletion of all data or samples where the protocol or law provides otherwise.
- Samples: what is collected, why, how long it is kept, where it may be transferred and what happens to remaining material; optional future use is a separate choice.
- Optional choices are visibly separate from the main decision and refusing them does not affect main participation.
- Signatures happen only during trial conduct: the participant or legally designated representative and the person conducting the consent interview sign and personally date before enrolment, and a copy of the signed form and the information is given. The CTIS copies are blank review versions. Witness fields only where the witness pathway applies; no invented signature blocks.
- Each document carries its title, version and date (in the footer); Bulgarian and English versions match.
- Risk frequency categories: the Bulgaria guides name none; use the categories of the IB or SmPC and record them as a source (DECISIONS.md D10).
- Participant templates (JSON): bold 'Инструкция за автора' paragraphs, 'Условен блок' labels, [square-bracket] notes (for example [попълнете], [population label], [date]), examples and unselected alternatives are drafting instructions; follow them and remove them. Underscore lines (____) for the consent visit stay blank. Part II templates (DOCX): yellow [INSERT/IF/OTHERWISE/CHOOSE/NOTE] labels. Nothing of either may remain in a final document.
