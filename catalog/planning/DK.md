  national_notes: 'CTIS title: K1_Recruitment arrangements_DK_da. Not needed if: recruitment.procedure_fully_in_protocol (the information is fully contained in the protocol; DECISIONS.md D3)

    Signed at CTIS submission: no

    Detailed description of subject inclusion and recruitment (Annex I) plus the Danish additions from VMK''s Part II guidance (September 2026): patient-record screening and contact rules, who gives oral information and receives consent (a duly qualified physician with Danish authorisation), delegation, validated translations and oral language support, and every population pathway in the plan (minors, adults without capacity, impartial witness, emergency).

    National additions to the form: - **v100**: Denmark: if patient records are used to identify potential participants, state the specific record data, the purpose, the approximate number of records and the time period covered, and the Danish legal basis used for the disclosure (the guides do not name it; take it from the sponsor, never from memory). Contact with a potential participant based on record information only with permission from the treating healthcare professional; if that person cannot be contacted, site management may give the permission described in the national guidance.

    - **v101**: Denmark: if recruitment begins from patient records rather than public advertising, the Danish patient-record rules (v100) apply to the first act of recruitment as well.

    - **v102**: Denmark: advertisements and other recruitment communications are submitted when used, with a description of how responses are handled. Decentralised-trial materials are submitted in CTIS as presented, including images; audio and video cannot be uploaded as files, so a scene-by-scene storyboard with the text or speech is submitted in CTIS. VMK does not accept these materials outside CTIS.

    - **v105**: Denmark: patient-record screening needs the detail in v100 (data, purpose, approximate number of records, period, Danish legal basis) and the contact-permission rule (treating healthcare professional, fallback site management). The version actually used with Danish participants must be understandable to them; a non-Danish version for a participant who does not understand Danish follows the same translation-quality principle.

    - **v106**: Denmark: the oral information interview is performed by a duly qualified medical doctor with Danish authorisation who has appropriate clinical-trial and GCP experience and knowledge of the specific trial. If a potential participant or legally designated representative does not understand Danish, a validated written translation of both the participant information and the consent form into the person''s native language or another language understood to the same degree (validation: confirmation that the translation is understood as intended; the sponsor is responsible for the translation); the oral conversation is supported by a certified interpreter or another person fluent in the language where appropriate (for example the informing physician, other trial staff or a relative, depending on the case). The investigator remains responsible for understanding. Adequate time to decide.

    - **v107**: Denmark: normally the same Danish-authorised physician gives the oral information and receives consent. If the participant takes time to decide, another qualified, delegated physician may receive consent later and attest separately, provided that physician can answer any remaining questions. If the physician is not the principal investigator, a written agreement with the principal investigator is in place. Consent is written, dated and signed by the participant or legally designated representative and by the physician who performed the interview; the participant or representative receives a copy of the document by which consent was given. Understanding is verified during the interview.

    - **v108**: Denmark: the ethics consent to trial participation is not GDPR consent; the main trial consent form is not used to regulate ordinary data-law matters, and any distinct GDPR consent is clearly separated from the CTR trial consent. VMK''s CTR standard consent forms are used where the sponsor follows the national standard (DECISIONS.md D2); if their standard text fields are unchanged and the sponsor confirms that, VMK accepts the form without comments on those fields. If records are used for follow-up after withdrawal, state and justify the purpose, extent and conditions. Where significant health findings are reasonably likely, describe the Danish feedback process and the separate right-not-to-know form.

    - **v110**: Denmark: adult without capacity not under guardianship: surrogate consent from both the nearest relative and a trial guardian (forsøgsværge); valid only when both have consented in writing. The nearest relative is determined by the participant''s actual relationship and circumstances (for example spouse or cohabiting partner, direct-line relatives and siblings, in some situations another family member or a friend with regular close contact), not by a sponsor-defined hierarchy. The trial guardian is a physician with relevant knowledge of the research area who safeguards the participant''s interests and is independent of the investigator''s interests and of the trial; describe how the trial guardian is selected and how independence is kept. Adult under guardianship: the legal guardian consents only if the guardianship covers consent to research participation; the site confirms the mandate before relying on the signature. The participant is informed as far as possible; a genuine current refusal (verbal or by behaviour, body language or resistance) is respected and not overridden by the surrogate; describe how objection is assessed throughout the trial. When capacity returns, the participant''s own consent as soon as they can receive the information, and how this transition is recognised and documented. The Danish-authorised physician rule applies to the representative consent process.

    - **v111**: Denmark: the child is informed and involved as far as age and maturity allow, with age-appropriate written information where the child can understand it and an adapted oral explanation; the child''s wishes are given weight and parental consent does not permit research against the child''s will (opposition may be verbal or shown by behaviour or resistance). The child''s agreement is not a substitute for parental consent. Describe how the team responds if a child or adolescent refuses a procedure or wishes to stop, especially with repeated invasive procedures.

    - **v112**: Denmark: the person conducting the participant information process in a CTR medicinal trial meets the Danish medical-doctor requirement (duly qualified physician with Danish authorisation); for 15-17-year-olds the same physician gives the oral information and receives the young person''s consent, subject to the delegated-physician rule in v107.

    - **v113**: Denmark: when the participant reaches legal adulthood (18) during the trial, new written informed consent for continued participation is obtained after the age of legal competence is reached and before further trial-specific actions, without harmful interruption of treatment or care (CTR Q&A 7.3); plan the timing rather than waiting for close-out. A participant who turns 15 during the trial gives own written consent under the Danish rule.

    - **v114**: Denmark: under 15, consent from the holder or holders of parental authority (forældremyndighedsindehaver); with joint parental authority both consent (a parental-authority power of attorney where the approved Danish route uses it). 15 to 17 and able to consent: the young person''s own written consent together with consent from the parent or parents holding parental authority (VMK dedicated CTR form for 15-17-year-olds and parents). If a 15-17-year-old is temporarily unable to consent, the parental surrogate pathway, then the young person''s own consent when able. Exceptions in general Danish research guidance for low-burden non-drug projects are not relied on unless VMK has expressly confirmed that they apply to this CTR trial.

    - **v115**: Denmark: the CTR Article 29 witness pathway is used only when the participant or legally designated representative can make the informed decision but cannot write; not for incapacity (legal-representative pathway) or emergency inclusion (Article 35). State why the normal written-signature process cannot be used.

    - **v116**: Denmark: describe the alternative method to give and record consent, how the impartial witness is selected (independent of the decision, able to attest that the alternative process was carried out as described, no role conflicting with the participant''s interests; state the independence rationale), how the witness is present, what is recorded in the source documentation and how the final ICF or consent record is retained. There is no Danish national witness form in the VMK CTR library.

    - **v117**: Denmark: the witness signs and dates the consent document; the Danish-authorised physician who performed the interview also signs and dates; the same physician gives the oral information and receives the consent, with a written agreement with the principal investigator if that physician is not the PI. The participant or representative receives a copy. Validated translations and oral language support where Danish is not understood. If the need arises unexpectedly after authorisation, check whether the approved documents already permit the alternative means; otherwise assess the CTIS modification route first.

    - **v118**: Denmark (VMK emergency-trial guidance 2026): show every Article 35 element: genuine urgency with a sudden life-threatening or other sudden serious condition and the therapeutic window; scientific grounds for a direct clinically relevant benefit; prior consent impossible within the window; no known prior objection and how prior objections are checked (for example the medical record); the trial directly concerns the emergency condition and can only be done in that setting; minimal additional risk and burden compared with standard treatment, with a realistic risk assessment. Lack of time alone is not enough.

    - **v119**: Denmark: describe how the emergency situation is identified and documented, which participant categories may be included and which legally designated representatives apply to each: adult without capacity not under guardianship, both the nearest relative and the trial guardian (valid only when both have consented in writing; the trial guardian is an independent physician with relevant knowledge, not part of the trial team and without conflicting interests); minor, the holder or holders of parental authority (both if joint), plus the 15-17-year-old''s own consent if able; adult under guardianship, the guardian if the guardianship covers research consent. Subsequent consent is sought as soon as possible and without undue delay, with an estimate of when consent-seeking starts for each participant group; written and oral information, time to ask questions and consider; every attempt is documented.

    - **v120**: Denmark: when the participant regains capacity (or a 15-17-year-old becomes able), their own consent to continued participation is obtained then, by the Danish-authorised physician (delegated-physician rule as in v107).

    - **v121**: Denmark: if subsequent consent is refused or cannot be obtained, the participant cannot continue: trial treatment, trial-specific activities, new data collection and new access to the medical record for trial data stop. Data collected up to that point may continue to be used under the CTR and data-protection framework; the participant or representative is told about the possibility of objecting to use of already collected data. Describe this pathway before the trial starts.

    Completed by: assistant

# National rules – Denmark

## Authorities and fee

- **competent authority**: Lægemiddelstyrelsen (Danish Medicines Agency). Coordinates the national assessment with VMK and issues a joint Danish decision through CTIS; its inspectors may access medical records for inspection of clinical trials.
- **ethics**: De Videnskabsetiske Medicinske Komitéer (VMK, the Medical Research Ethics Committees) assess Part II using Annex I and VMK's national Part II guidance (updated September 2026). Denmark has not created a separate national CTR checklist replacing Annex I.
- **communication**: Communication with the authorities may be in Danish or English; VMK treats Danish as the national language for participant-facing information.
- **insurance**: The sponsor is responsible for coverage at all Danish sites: Patienterstatningen (Danish Patient Compensation) or independent insurance.
- **data protection**: GDPR and the Danish Data Protection Act (databeskyttelsesloven); complaints to Datatilsynet (supplied templates).
- **fee**: One combined fee for the Danish Medicines Agency and VMK, paid to the Danish Medicines Agency no later than one month after receipt of the invoice; not a pre-submission requirement unless the current invoice process for the application says otherwise (DK-FEE).
- **consent professional role**: Oral information and receipt of consent by a duly qualified physician with Danish authorisation (population_rules.consent_taker).

## Populations, age bands and representatives

- **authority**: De Videnskabsetiske Medicinske Komitéer (VMK): Guidance for preparation of Part II (updated September 2026), CTR and CTIS questions and answers (updated September 2026), CTR consent forms, General guidance on informed and surrogate consent (September 2024), Guidance on clinical trials in emergency situations under CTR (2026), Guidance on compensation to research participants; European Commission CTR Q&A version 7.3 (July 2026); Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 Articles 28 to 35. As recorded in the TrialAgents Denmark guides reviewed 23 September 2026.
- **consent taker**: A duly qualified medical doctor with Danish authorisation, with appropriate clinical-trial and GCP experience and knowledge of the trial, gives the oral information and receives consent. Normally the same physician; after a reflection period another qualified, delegated physician may receive consent and attest separately, provided that physician can answer remaining questions. Written agreement with the principal investigator when the physician is not the PI. Witness route: the same physician gives the information and receives the consent.
- **minors**:
  - **age bands**:

    - **template**: DK-PT-age-6-11
    - **reader**: child
    - **legal role**: information and record of the child's agreement or objection; not legal consent
    - **band**: 12-14
    - **template**: DK-PT-age-12-17
    - **route**: A
    - **reader**: adolescent
    - **legal role**: information and record of agreement or objection; parents give the legal consent
    - **band**: 15-17
    - **template**: DK-PT-age-12-17
    - **route**: B
    - **reader**: adolescent
    - **legal role**: own written informed consent together with the parents' consent (when able to consent)
  - **always**:
    - DK-PT-parents
  - **band basis**: 15 is the authority's threshold (VMK: a capable 15-17-year-old gives own consent together with the holders of parental authority; dedicated 15-17 PIS and consent form). Below 15 VMK sets no band: age-appropriate written information where the child can understand it, following the protocol age bands. 5-11 and 12-14 are the templates' drafting bands.
  - **band conflicts**:
    - The adolescent template covers 12-17 in one file; the authority treats 15-17 as its own consent pathway with a dedicated PIS and consent form. Authority wins: one document per route (12-14 route A, 15-17 route B), never one combined 12-17 document.
    - The children's template file name says 6-11; its description and instructions say 5-11. VMK sets no band, so this is not an authority conflict; the file name is kept as supplied and the band 5-11 from the template text is used (DECISIONS.md D5).
  - **parental authority**: Both holders consent when parental authority is joint; one signature only with sole parental authority, a valid written power of attorney (population set parental_power_of_attorney) or another documented approved basis.
  - **turning 15**: Own written consent with the 15-17 document (route B) when the participant turns 15 and is able to consent.
  - **reconsent at 18**: DK-PT-main or DK-PT-healthy: new written consent after reaching 18 and before further trial-specific actions, without harmful interruption of treatment or care (CTR Q&A 7.3).
  - **under 5**: No template. Information adapted to the child as far as age and maturity allow; written information only where the child can understand it; opposition respected (DECISIONS.md D5).
  - **information giver**: The Danish medical-doctor requirement applies to the participant information process in a CTR medicinal trial, also for minors; the templates' 'person with pedagogical prerequisites' supports but does not replace the physician (templates/README.md, conflict 3; DECISIONS.md D7).
  - **dissent**: A minor is not forced to participate against a current objection, verbal or non-verbal; the consent procedure explains how refusal of a procedure or a wish to stop is handled.
  - **exceptions**: Exceptions in general Danish research guidance for low-burden non-drug projects (for example a dispensation from parental consent) are not relied on unless VMK has expressly confirmed that they apply to the CTR trial; the 12-17 template's dispensation alternative is deleted by default.

  - Each consent pathway has its own document set; the competent-adult form is never used for a legal-representative pathway because it is easier to adapt (DK-ICF).
  - The participant information (DK-L1-SIS) and the blank consent form (DK-L1-ICF) are separate uploads with the same version and date (DECISIONS.md D1).
  - Minors: parental-authority information and consent, age-appropriate child information for younger participants who can understand it, a dedicated 15-17 information sheet and consent form when that age group is enrolled, the parental-authority power of attorney where used (DK-MINORS).
  - Adults without capacity: representative information and consent with the nearest relative and the trial guardian as distinct roles, plus adapted participant information (DK-CAPACITY).
  - A defined optional substudy has its own PIS and ICF in Part II (DK-SECONDARY).
  - Article 28(2) secondary data use needs a separate consent form from the main ICF (DK-SECONDARY).
  - The right-not-to-know declaration is a separate form (DK-ICF).
  - Do not create a separate form simply because a global master package contains one; the question is whether the document belongs to the Danish Part II approval and which consent pathway it supports (DK-QC#4).

## Drafting rules

- Participant documents: keep the supplied CTIS_DK templates' structure and model text (their consent declarations follow the VMK CTR standard wording); add all study-specific information; record every deviation as a finding. Where the sponsor follows the VMK standard form, keep its standard text fields unchanged so the sponsor can confirm that to VMK (DECISIONS.md D2).
- Concise, clear language suitable for a lay reader; explain what the protocol means for a Danish participant rather than copying protocol language; define unavoidable clinical terms at first use (DK-ICF, DK-QC#5).
- Consent physician: the participant documents and K1 say that a duly qualified physician with Danish authorisation gives the oral information and receives consent. The newest VMK guidance (September 2026) allows a later qualified, delegated physician to receive consent after a reflection period and attest separately; the templates say the same physician must receive consent. The guidance wins: when the protocol or K1 uses that route, add a separate attestation block for the later physician and adjust the sentence, visibly as a deviation (templates/README.md, conflict 1; DECISIONS.md D4). The witness route keeps the same physician.
- Record access wording (mandatory when records are accessed): sponsor, sponsor representatives and investigator may obtain and process necessary health information from the medical record and supplementing systems for trial conduct, quality control and monitoring; Danish Medicines Agency inspectors may access necessary health information; foreign medicines authorities where relevant. Record-based follow-up after withdrawal: extent, conditions and purpose, consistent with the protocol.
- Privacy: understandable privacy information and data-subject rights; the ethics consent to participation is not GDPR consent and the main ICF does not regulate ordinary data-law matters; any distinct GDPR consent clearly separated. Processing or transfer outside Denmark: purpose of each; outside the EU/EEA: the countries and compliance with GDPR Chapter V. No separate yes/no choice only for a transfer (template rule).
- Biological material: types, number or amount per participant, purpose of each, analysis place, storage place and duration, purpose of storage, destruction after analysis or at trial end, transfers outside Denmark (third countries with transfer information). Archived material used in the trial: inform the participant; no separate consent solely for that. Research biobank where material is stored beyond about 5-7 days (template).
- Significant health findings, where reasonably likely: possibility of findings, the Danish feedback approach and the separate right-not-to-know form after information about the consequences.
- Surplus trial material for unspecified future research: only the VMK opt-out checkbox on the main consent form; no other future-research boxes on the main ICF. Optional substudy: its own PIS and ICF. Article 28(2) secondary data use: separate consent form, withdrawable.
- State clearly when no direct benefit is expected; compensation, reimbursement and the Patienterstatningen or insurance wording match P1, P2 and O1; compensation must not improperly drive the decision.
- Availability of trial results as required by the CTR (results in CTIS under the EU trial number).
- Version number and date consistent across the PIS and the ICF; blank, unsigned submission copies; no legacy non-CTR consent form substituted for the CTR form; optional checkboxes do not create consent logic VMK does not accept; internal references to protocol sections, appendices and contacts correct (DK-QC#9).
- Risk frequency: the Denmark guides set no categories; the templates use numeric or plain categories when reliable data exist (for example 'hyppig / 1-10 %'). Use the categories of the IB or SmPC until the user decides (DECISIONS.md D16).
- Enclose or refer to the current Danish information 'Dine rettigheder som forsøgsperson i forsøg med medicin' as the templates instruct (DECISIONS.md D14).
- Participant templates (JSON): <i>[Redaktionsnote: …]</i> notes, other [square-bracket] alternatives and gaps such as [indsæt], legacy ' FORMTEXT ' field prompts and non-selected routes are drafting instructions; follow and remove them, and keep fields completed by hand at consent blank. Part II templates (DOCX): yellow [INSERT/IF/OTHERWISE/CHOOSE/NOTE: …] labels. Nothing of either may remain in a final document.
