    Completed by: assistant

    CTIS Partie II, section K (Recruitment arrangements). Document obligatoire, à déposer avec la demande initiale. Rédigé en français ; réponses du formulaire en français. Aucune signature lors du dépôt CTIS. Utiliser le modèle européen harmonisé « Recruitment and Informed Consent Procedure » version 2.0 (applicable aux nouvelles soumissions de la Partie II à partir du 1er septembre 2026). Décrire chaque voie d''inclusion du protocole : identification, première approche, information, délai de réflexion, vérification de la compréhension, version signée, recueil du consentement écrit (participant et investigateur ou personne qui mène l''entretien). Mineurs : vérification de l''autorité parentale, information par tranche d''âge, recherche de l''adhésion personnelle, enregistrement du refus, nouveau consentement à 18 ans. Majeurs protégés : évaluation de la capacité, vérification du statut juridique (curatelle, tutelle ou autre mesure avec représentation, absence de mesure) et de l''habilitation, implication du participant, suivi de l''opposition, consentement ultérieur. Témoin : quand la voie s''applique, distinction entre incapacité d''écrire et défaut de capacité, choix du tiers, mode d''expression du consentement, signataires. Urgence : reproduire la procédure opérationnelle de l''article 35 (vérification de l''absence d''opposition préalable, indisponibilité du représentant, autres conditions) ; une simple phrase du protocole sur le consentement différé ne suffit pas. Volontaires sains : identification, sélection, délai de réflexion, indemnisation, période d''exclusion et processus de consentement. Si aucun support de recrutement séparé n''est prévu, l''indiquer clairement (voir FR-K2). Consentement électronique ou à distance : décrire l''information, l''identification, la signature, la copie et l''archivage.

    National additions to the form: - **v105**: Required in France: in French, state how recruitment material is handled (every participant-facing item filed before use; new or materially changed items through a CTIS modification; or a clear statement that no separate advertising is used) and, for healthy volunteers, identification, screening, decision time, compensation and any exclusion period (also stated in the Additional France application document).

    - **v108**: Required in France: in French, state that the participant and the investigator or other person conducting the consent interview sign and date the approved French form before any trial-specific procedure, that the participant receives a copy, that the consent record identifies the information version, that a genetic examination needs separate express written prior consent (Civil Code Article 16-10) in a separate document, and how any electronic or remote consent meets the same information, identity, signature, copy and record requirements.

    - **v110**: Required in France when adults lacking capacity are included: in French, describe the route by verified protective status (Article L1122-2 CSP): curatorship, where the participant consents with the curator''s assistance; guardianship or another measure with representation, where the authorised representative authorises within the scope of the order or mandate; adult unable to consent without a formal measure, where authorisation is sought from the trusted person, otherwise a family member, otherwise a person with close and stable ties. Persons under sauvegarde de justice are not enrolled. Judge or family-council authorisation where the CPP considers there is a serious risk to private life or bodily integrity. Personal adherence is sought; an explicit refusal cannot be overridden; own consent when capacity returns.

    - **v114**: Required in France when minors are included: in French, state that both holders of parental authority authorise and sign where two hold it; the one-holder exception only when all statutory conditions are met (minimal foreseeable risk and burden, the minor is not a healthy volunteer, the other holder cannot authorise in a time compatible with the trial) and is documented; authority is verified from evidence, not inferred from family relationship; information is adapted to age and maturity by trained or experienced staff; the minor''s personal adherence is sought and a refusal cannot be overridden; own adult consent when the participant reaches 18 (Article L1122-2 CSP, Article 32 CTR).

    - **v117**: Required in France when an impartial witness may be used: in French, state that the attesting person is the participant''s trusted person, a family member or a close person, independent of the investigator and sponsor (Article L1122-1-1 CSP); not a member of the trial team, sponsor staff or anyone with a trial interest; present for the information and the expression of consent; relationship and independence documented; interpreter and witness roles not combined unless the approved procedure permits it.

    - **v121**: Required in France when emergency enrolment is used: in French, describe the Article 35 checks before the first intervention (prior objection, representative availability, therapeutic window, direct-benefit basis, minimal risk and burden), the source record of each condition, information and consent of the participant without undue delay, authorisation by the legally designated representative as soon as available, the participant''s own consent if capacity returns, and information on the right to object to use of data already obtained (Article L1122-1-3 CSP).

    Template basis: Harmonised EU Recruitment and Informed Consent Procedure Template, version 2.0 (applies to new Part II submissions from 1 September 2026)'

# National rules – France

## Authorities and fee

- **competent authority**: ANSM (Agence nationale de sécurité du médicament et des produits de santé) assesses Part I.
- **ethics**: A Comité de protection des personnes (CPP), allocated nationally when the application is deposited in CTIS, assesses Part II (recruitment, information and consent, investigator and site suitability, insurance, financial arrangements, data protection, biological samples). Part II questions are handled through CTIS; responses are normally filed in French; a question on a CV or declaration of interest may be answered in that document's permitted language.
- **data protection**: CNIL: declaration of conformity with MR-001 (receipt filed in CTIS), or CNIL authorisation after the CPP opinion for non-compliant processing.
- **national guidance**: CNRIPH publishes the national France recommendations and the Additional France application document; French Ministry of Health list of documents expected for Part II (English version 18 July 2025); ANSM sponsor notice for CTIS Part II applications (11 February 2026).
- **regional**: Agence régionale de santé (ARS): site authorisation only where the research requires acts not usually performed at the site (Article L1121-13 CSP).
- **fee**: France charges no clinical trial application fee under the current CTIS pathway; no proof of fee payment is submitted (section Q).

## Language rules

- **participant documents**: fr
- **administrative documents**:
  - fr
- **investigator documents**:
  - fr
  - en
- **rule**: The France Part II dossier is prepared in French. Participant-facing documents and most France-specific Part II documents must be in French, even when much of Part I is in English. Investigator CVs and declarations of interest may be in French or English.
- **rfi responses**: Normally in French; a request for information on a CV or declaration of interest may use the same permitted language as that document.
- **english source**: The English source may support drafting and sponsor review, but the CPP reviews the French participant version. Translate all participant-facing appendices, diagrams, questionnaires and digital screens.
- **form answers**: Part II forms (shared EU templates in content/eu/templates/part-ii) are answered in French; M1 and M2 are completed by the investigator in French or English.

## Populations, age bands and representatives

- **authority**: No national template or statutory age bands. Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 Articles 28 to 35; French Public Health Code Articles L1122-1, L1122-1-1, L1122-1-3, L1122-2, L1121-11, R1121-16; Civil Code Articles 16-10 and 388; national Part II guidance (French Ministry of Health list, 18 July 2025); CNIL MR-001 (May 2026); as recorded in the France guides reviewed 23 September 2026.
- **minors**:
  - **age bands**:
    - **band**: 6-11
    - **template**: FR-PT-age-6-11
    - **reader**: child
    - **band**: 12-17
    - **template**: FR-PT-age-12-17
    - **reader**: adolescent
  - **always**:
    - FR-PT-parents
  - **band rule**: France sets no statutory child or adolescent bands. The template bands (6-11, 12-17) are drafting bands; per study they must be justified by readability and maturity. A single age-adapted document may be enough for a narrow cohort; a broad paediatric range usually needs more than one version.
  - **band conflict**: None: the national authority sets no band, so the supplied 6-11 band is kept (unlike Germany, where AKEK's 7-11 replaced 6-11).
  - **reconsent at 18**: FR-PT-main, when participants can reach 18 during the trial; plan the timing in K1.
  - **under 6**: No template. French information adapted to each minor who can understand any part of the decision is mandatory; use a developmentally appropriate format and record the minor's response. A refusal cannot be overridden (DECISIONS.md, Missing).
  - **parental authorisation**: Both holders of parental authority authorise and sign where two hold it. One holder only when all statutory conditions are met (minimal foreseeable risk and burden, not a healthy volunteer, other holder unable to authorise in time), documented with the reason. Authority verified from evidence.
  - **payment**: No constraint indemnity for minors; only reimbursement of justified expenses.
  - **note**: Healthy-volunteer forms are not adapted into a minor pathway.
- **adults without capacity**:
  - **always**:
    - FR-PT-legal-rep
    - FR-PT-limited-capacity
  - **routes**:
    - **curatorship**: Participant consents with the curator's assistance; adapted participant document with participant and curator-assistance signature fields (population set adults_under_curatorship; FR-PT-legal-rep must not be used).
    - **representation measure**: Guardianship, future-protection mandate or family authorisation with representation: the authorised representative authorises within the scope of the order or mandate (FR-PT-legal-rep + FR-PT-limited-capacity).
    - **no formal measure**: Trusted person, otherwise family member, otherwise person with close and stable ties; document why the participant could not consent and the absence of a higher-priority person (FR-PT-legal-rep + FR-PT-limited-capacity).
    - **sauvegarde de justice**: Excluded from this interventional research route.
    - **serious risk**: Judge or family-council authorisation where the CPP considers there is a serious risk to private life or bodily integrity (conditional, retained in the site file).
  - **regained capacity**: FR-PT-main for the participant's own consent without undue delay.
  - **payment**: No constraint indemnity where French law prohibits it for the category; reimbursement of justified expenses only.
  - **note**: An explicit refusal or withdrawal by the participant cannot be overridden by the representative.
- **emergency**:
  - **always**:
    - FR-PT-emergency
    - FR-PT-legal-rep
  - **recommended**: Short French emergency information aid; site checklist or source-note template for the Article 35 conditions (internal). No templates supplied.
  - **note**: FR-PT-legal-rep is used for the representative's later authorisation when the participant remains unable to consent; FR-PT-main or the emergency form for the participant's own consent once capable.
- **impartial witness**:
  - **always**:
    - FR-PT-witness
  - **note**: Capable participant unable to write. Witness independent of investigator and sponsor; never a trial-team member or sponsor staff.
- **healthy volunteers**:
  - **always**:
    - FR-PT-healthy
  - **note**: Exclusion period in the Additional France application document; national register information where it applies; constraint indemnity cap EUR 6,000 over twelve consecutive months.
- **pregnancy**:
  - **participant**:
    - FR-PT-pregnancy
  - **partner**:
    - FR-PT-pregnant-partner
  - **note**: Participant form conditional (when the main consent does not cover the follow-up); partner form mandatory when partner data are collected; parental-authority authorisation for identifiable infant follow-up.
- **shareable templates**:
  - FR-PT-pregnant-partner
  - FR-PT-secondary
- **shareable note**: May be one shared document for several populations when nothing in it would differ; agree with the user. Secondary-use authorisation for minors and protected adults follows the population's consent giver, so a separate version may be needed.
- **separate document rules**:
  - A genetic examination needs a separate French information and express written prior consent document (Civil Code Article 16-10; national guidance), not only a checkbox in the main form (FR-L1-GENETIC).
  - Create separate or clearly differentiated versions where the information, consent giver, procedures or risks differ.
  - Where an activity needs its own legally distinct consent, use a separate document rather than only an extra checkbox.
  - Secondary use outside the protocol: separate section or document; a separate document where the purpose, retention, recipients, sample governance or withdrawal needs substantial explanation.
  - A pregnant partner receives her own document; the participant cannot consent for her.
  - A changed sample purpose or materially different secondary use needs updated information and new consent before it begins.
  - Re-consent with the current adult French information at 18 and when capacity returns.

## Drafting rules

- France has no mandatory national template for participant documents: content and signature logic follow Article 29 CTR, the French Public Health Code, CNIL requirements and the national Part II guidance. The supplied TrialAgents French templates are the baseline; follow their content_instructions.
- French that a lay reader in the intended population understands: clear, concise and fair; excessive length can itself impair understanding. Short sentences, active voice, terms and abbreviations defined at first use, consistent wording for each concept. Do not use the expression « tomber enceinte » (templates).
- Structure per the templates: Part 1 information about the research; Part 2 participant rights and management of collected data; Part 3 glossary where useful; consent form visually separate.
- State that the project is research, not ordinary care; distinguish routine care from research-only procedures, risks and constraints.
- Identify the favourable CPP opinion and the ANSM authorisation, with wording that can be finalised once the decisions are available; include the EU trial number.
- Do not promise personal benefit; describe individual benefit only where reasonably possible and wider scientific benefit separately; balanced risks; alternatives including not participating; planned care after the trial.
- Data protection: controller, any EU representative and DPO; data categories and sources, purposes, legal basis (do not equate trial consent with the GDPR legal basis without the controller's analysis), recipients, retention, security, rights and CNIL complaint route; Article 13 for direct collection, Article 14 for other sources.
- Transfers: list the destination countries, recipients or recipient categories, the purpose of each transfer and whether the destination has an adequacy decision; a vague statement such as 'transfer outside Europe' is not enough.
- The participant notice matches the MR-001 or CNIL-authorisation route declared in the Additional France application document.
- Compensation: reimbursement of expenses and any constraint indemnity only; no other financial advantage; total indemnity capped at EUR 6,000 per person over twelve consecutive months; none for minors or protected persons where prohibited; amounts identical to P1 and the Additional France application document.
- Insurance: explain the mandatory insurance arrangements and the practical route for a research-related injury without turning the document into a policy summary.
- Withdrawal: distinguish withdrawal from the trial, objection to future data processing, destruction of remaining samples and withdrawal of an optional secondary-use choice; do not promise deletion the law or scientific integrity does not permit.
- Results and rights: the right to receive personal health information and, after the research, the overall results; when and how a results summary is available.
- Genetic examination: separate document with express written prior consent (FR-L1-GENETIC); a checkbox in the main form is not enough.
- Optional activities: separate yes/no choices; no bundled signature for unrelated permissions; optional elements are not conditions of entry unless genuinely part of the protocol and justified.
- Signatures: filed unsigned with final signature blocks for the participant and the investigator or person conducting the consent interview (plus parent, representative, curator, witness or separate data subject only where the route requires); roles labelled in French (authorisation, assistance, attestation or consent); no predating; traceable corrections; the signer receives a copy; the consent record identifies the information version.
- Every page carries a consistent title, version and date; searchable-text PDFs, no image-only PDFs.
- Translate all participant-facing appendices, diagrams, questionnaires and digital screens used to obtain or support consent; the CPP reviews the French version.
- Participant templates (JSON): italic text, [square-bracket] notes and "..........." gaps are drafting instructions or gaps; follow them and remove them. Part II templates (DOCX): yellow [INSERT/IF/OTHERWISE/CHOOSE/NOTE: …] labels. Nothing of either may remain in a final document.
