# National rules – Estonia

## Authorities and fee

- **competent authority**: State Agency of Medicines (Ravimiamet): performs the Member State functions under the CTR, assesses the application with experts and the Ethics Committee for Medicinal Products, and issues the national decision.
- **ethics**: One Ethics Committee for Medicinal Products for all medicinal-product trials (no choice between local committees). It prepares the Part II ethical assessment; the Agency takes its conclusion into account.
- **fee**: No prepayment of the specialised assessment fee. The sponsor provides invoice-recipient and decision-recipient details; Ravimiamet invoices within 10 days after the application is accepted for evaluation. Statutory exemptions for certain academic sponsors and orphan-medicinal-product trials (EE-FEE). The guides state no amount.
- **national guidance**: Estonia Medicinal Products Act (sections 99³ and 99⁵); Ravimiamet, Ethics Committee for Medicinal Products: Part II requirements, compensation and consent Q&A (updated 25 May 2026); Ravimiamet, Clinical Trials Regulation no 536/2014 page (updated 30 June 2026), including the CTIS instruction on Estonian patient-facing materials from 27 April 2026; Ravimiamet clinical-trial forms page with the Estonia SIS/ICF templates (Estonian and English); Ravimiamet clinical trials in Estonia page (Health Information System and monitoring); EudraLex Volume 10 including the July 2026 Q&A and Part II templates; ICH E6(R3) published by Ravimiamet as current GCP guidance.

## Language rules

- **participant documents**: et
- **administrative documents**:
  - en
  - et
- **rule**: The application dossier is generally submitted in English. Information specified in Part L of Annex I to the CTR and other information aimed at participants must be in Estonian (Medicinal Products Act; the Commission language table lists Estonian for translated patient-facing documents).

## Populations, age bands and representatives

- **authority**: State Agency of Medicines (Ravimiamet) and the Ethics Committee for Medicinal Products (Part II requirements, compensation and consent Q&A, updated 25 May 2026); Estonia Medicinal Products Act, sections 99³ and 99⁵; Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 Articles 28 to 35; ICH E6(R3); as recorded in the Estonia guides reviewed 23 September 2026.
- **template status**: No Estonian participant templates were supplied: participant_templates is empty and every template id below refers to participant_templates_missing ("template": "missing" in population_sets).
- **language sets**:
  - **et**: missing: every participant document is planned in Estonian.
  - **other**: Each other language intended for use with participants (for example Russian or English) is an extra version of the same document, titled _EE_<lang> (DECISIONS.md D2). The guides do not mention Russian.
- **minors**:
  - **age bands**:
    - **band**: 7-17
    - **template**: EE-PT-minor-7-17
    - **reader**: minor (child or adolescent)
    - **basis**: Medicinal Products Act section 99⁵: for a person aged 7 to 17 consent from the minor as well as the legal representative, taking the minor's intent into account. Statutory band; age-adapted versions inside it are drafting choices (DECISIONS.md D7).
    - **band**: under 7
    - **template**: EE-PT-child-under-7
    - **reader**: young child
    - **optional**: True
    - **basis**: No national co-consent rule below 7; a young-child information sheet may be appropriate when the child can understand simple information (DECISIONS.md D8).
  - **always**:
    - EE-PT-parents
  - **band rule**: 7 to 17 is the statutory co-consent band. Every age band in the protocol needs a matching information document; adapt to age and mental maturity, never shorten the adult form mechanically. Do not design the 7 to 17 document so that only the representative decides and the minor merely acknowledges receiving information.
  - **representatives**: The legally designated representative consents. The number of representative signatures depends on who is legally authorised in the specific case; no two-parent rule without a verified legal basis (DECISIONS.md D9).
  - **assent dissent**: An explicit wish of a minor capable of forming an opinion to refuse or withdraw is respected (Article 32). The representative's consent does not remove the requirement to involve the minor.
  - **reconsent at adulthood**: EE-PT-main or EE-PT-healthy when the participant reaches legal adulthood and ongoing participation still requires consent; the protocol and consent plan say when and how.
  - **payment**: Minors and their representatives receive no compensation for participation, only reimbursement of direct costs.
- **adults without capacity**:
  - **always**:
    - EE-PT-legal-rep
    - EE-PT-limited-capacity
  - **note**: Only when the participant cannot give valid consent and Article 31 permits inclusion. Representative consent takes presumed intent into account; a decision that clearly harms the participant's interests cannot be relied on. Diagnosis, disability or age alone is not proof of incapacity. Re-consent with EE-PT-main when capacity is regained; plan for fluctuating capacity. Only reimbursement of direct costs for participant and representative.
- **emergency**:
  - **always**:
    - EE-PT-emergency
  - **optional**:
    - EE-PT-legal-rep
  - **note**: Article 35 only. Consent to continue without undue delay from the representative (minors, incapacitated participants) or from the participant or representative, whichever is available first. No national emergency form.
- **impartial witness**:
  - **always**:
    - EE-PT-witness
  - **note**: When the participant or representative cannot write (Article 29) or, under ICH E6(R3), cannot read. Not for disability or limited literacy alone when a valid process is possible without it. A witness cannot cure an otherwise invalid pathway; minor and capacity rules still apply.
- **pregnancy**:
  - **participant**: EE-PT-pregnancy when the protocol requires pregnancy follow-up of an enrolled participant beyond the main form
  - **partner**: EE-PT-pregnant-partner whenever identifiable health information about a participant's pregnant partner is collected
  - **payment**: Pregnant and breastfeeding participants receive only reimbursement of direct costs; do not extend this rule to a non-participant partner without assessing the legal and ethics position.
- **secondary use**:
  - **always**:
    - EE-PT-secondary
  - **note**: Separate consent choices for trial participation and for use of pseudonymised health data or biological samples (Ravimiamet). No bundled 'I agree to everything' signature. Ethical consent is not automatically the GDPR legal basis.
- **compensation**:
  - **all participants**: Unavoidable expenses directly caused by trial participation are reimbursed; the application justifies them and the ICF explains the principles. Compensation information is in the ICF, never in recruitment material.
  - **competent adults**: Justified compensation for inconvenience and burdensome procedures allowed when justified in the application and approved by the Ethics Committee; reflects time and contribution (healthy volunteers: time based on the Estonian minimum wage in the year the trial was initiated); not payment for risk; no completion bonus or held-back share.
  - **restricted**: Minors and their representatives, incapacitated participants and their representatives, pregnant women and breastfeeding women: only reimbursement of direct costs.
- **shareable templates**:
  - EE-PT-pregnant-partner
  - EE-PT-secondary
- **separate document rules**:
  - The default adult form is not enough for every population: healthy volunteers, minors, incapacitated adults, emergency enrolment, impartial witness, pregnancy follow-up and secondary use need additional consent logic, not a copy of the adult form (Estonia hub and patient information guide).
  - Patients and healthy volunteers in one trial: a dedicated healthy-volunteer document.
  - Minors aged 7 to 17: the minor's own consent and the representative's consent, each decision and signature clear.
  - The pregnant partner receives her own document; the participant's main form is not her consent.
  - Optional future use of data or samples is a separate choice from main-trial participation.
  - Re-consent with the adult form when a minor reaches adulthood or an incapacitated participant regains capacity.

## Drafting rules

- Participant documents are in plain Estonian suitable for the target population; every other language intended for use is provided as a complete version of the same document. Headings explain the decision being made; abbreviations defined; risks not hidden in dense paragraphs.
- Use the term 'participant' (Estonian 'uuringus osaleja'), not 'patient', throughout participant materials (Ethics Committee guidance).
- Every participant-facing item carries the EU CT number, together with a clear version number, date, consistent trial identifier, sponsor name, site name where site-specific and the correct language label.
- Start from the Estonia national SIS/ICF template (Ravimiamet, approved by the Ethics Committee) or the EC Part II templates, and adapt to the actual intervention, procedures, population, risks, data flows, samples, reimbursement and optional research. The template is a baseline, not a substitute for protocol-specific content.
- Cover: purpose and why the person is invited; investigational treatment, comparator and randomisation; visits, tests, procedures, samples and participant responsibilities; foreseeable risks, burdens and inconveniences; expected benefits, including when no direct benefit is expected; alternatives; pregnancy, contraception and reproductive precautions; samples; personal and health data; insurance and trial-related injury; reimbursement and approved compensation; voluntariness, withdrawal and what happens after withdrawal; contacts for study questions, safety concerns and participant rights; optional future research as a separate decision.
- Every statement matches the current protocol and other trial documents (visit counts, time commitments, treatment periods, follow-up, blood volumes, imaging, biopsies, washouts, prohibited medicines, pregnancy testing); no procedure, benefit or risk statement the sources do not support. Participants receive only information relevant to the pathway they may enter.
- Consent: before consent the participant or legal representative speaks with a trial-team member who is a doctor, nurse or pharmacist with thorough knowledge of the trial and the IMP; adequate time to decide; written, dated and signed by the participant or representative and by the person conducting the interview; a digital signature valid under Estonian law is allowed; the participant receives a copy.
- Remote consent only by video call with electronic identification, with clear identification, normal questioning, time to decide and the same rights as face to face, a legally accepted signature, and prior justification and approval by the Ethics Committee.
- Data protection explained in detail and as the actual data flow: data categories, who collects and controls, who may access coded source data, pseudonymisation, transfers outside Estonia or the EEA, retention, monitoring and audit access, withdrawal consequences, optional future research, rights and contacts. Trial consent is not presented as the GDPR legal basis for all processing.
- Separate consent choices for trial participation and for use of pseudonymised health data or biological samples; the main-trial consent is not conditioned on optional research, genetics, sample storage or data use unless the protocol and law genuinely require it.
- Source-data access wording is realistic: sponsor representatives do not automatically gain direct access to Estonia's national Health Information System; trial-site systems determine what access can be provided.
- Samples: for each sample type state what is collected, purpose, coding status, transport, storage location, retention in years, destruction, genetic analysis, whether future use is optional and whether samples may leave Estonia or the EEA; consistent with S1.
- Compensation and reimbursement as in population_rules.compensation: reimbursement principles in the ICF; no completion bonus or deferred large share; restricted groups receive only direct-cost reimbursement.
- Withdrawal wording is realistic: distinguish stopping new optional use, retention required for safety, reliability or legal obligations, data already used and irreversibly anonymised material.
- Blank documents in CTIS: no pre-signed participant signature, no participant personal data, no tracked changes or drafting comments; signature blocks match the actual consent pathway.
- Participant templates were not supplied; where a reference document or a later template is used, its instructions ([square-bracket] notes, italic notes, '...........' gaps) and Part II form instructions (yellow [INSERT/IF/OTHERWISE/CHOOSE/NOTE: …] labels) are drafting instructions; none may remain in a final document.
