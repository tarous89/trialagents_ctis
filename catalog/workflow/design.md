General rules for a clear, well-designed document. The styling instructions for the specific document and country come first where they disagree.

- Start with the study information table (`table`, style `info`, no header row): study title, short title, protocol number with version and date, EU trial number, sponsor, and for participant documents the principal investigator and site.
- One section per topic, with a short heading in the reader's words ("What happens at each visit?"). Sections become shaded bands in the default layout.
- Short paragraphs of two to four sentences. Put the most important sentence first.
- Use a numbered list for steps in order, a bullet list for options or rights, and a table when readers compare items (visits × procedures, risks × frequency).
- Show a sequence of visits or phases as a `timeline` (two to eight steps, each with a short label and detail), followed by a table if the detail matters.
- Use a note (`info`, `warning` or `summary`) for what must not be missed: a warning for safety or pregnancy, a summary box for key facts at the start of long participant documents.
- Mark everything that is missing with [[...]] in the document's language ("[[nombre del investigador principal no facilitado]]"), so it is highlighted for the team.
- Use **bold** sparingly for key words and __underline__ only where the template or country asks for it. No colours or fonts of your own: the Word template carries the design.
- Fill the title, subtitle (population or variant) and both footer lines, in the document's language.
