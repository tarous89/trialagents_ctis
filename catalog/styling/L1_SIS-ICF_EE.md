Applies on top of `content/styles/base.md`. Sources: the Estonia guides (reviewed 23 September 2026): patient information and consent requirements, patient-facing document QC checklist and the population guides. No Estonian participant templates were supplied, so there are no template `styling_instructions`; the national rules below apply to every participant document. Where they are stricter than the base style, they win.

## Rules for all Estonian participant documents

- Written in plain Estonian suitable for the target participant population. Every other language in which a document will be used with participants (for example Russian or English) is a complete version with the same layout, version and date, and its own language label (DECISIONS.md D2).
- The term "participant" (Estonian "uuringus osaleja") is used throughout, not "patient".
- The EU CT number appears on every participant-facing item: in the `Info table` at the start and in the running footer (base style), together with version number, date, protocol number, population and language label. Page numbering in Estonian.
- Trial identity at the start (`Info table`): trial title, protocol number, sponsor, EU CT number, and the site name where the document is site-specific.
- Headings explain the decision being made; abbreviations are defined at first use; risks are not hidden in dense paragraphs. Visit schedules and procedure lists as tables (base style).
- Voluntariness and withdrawal, the main risks and what to do about safety concerns in `Info/warning/note box` elements where they help.
- Contacts block: contacts for study questions, safety concerns and participant rights.
- Reimbursement and any compensation have their own section in the information and consent form (they never appear in recruitment material).
- Data protection has its own detailed section describing the actual data flow.
- Consent choices: one separate choice for trial participation and separate choices for use of pseudonymised health data or biological samples and for each other optional activity (`Checkbox group`), never preselected. Optional choices are visually separate from mandatory trial participation.
- Signature section: participant or legal representative and the person conducting the consent interview, each with name, signature and date; the layout supports a digital signature valid under Estonian law. Witness, representative and minor blocks only in the pathway where they apply, clearly separated. No pre-signed signature on the blank submission version.
- Statement that the participant or representative receives a copy of the consent record.
- Clean submission version: no tracked changes or drafting comments; correct language label.

## Population-specific rules

- **Minors aged 7 to 17:** information adapted to age and mental maturity, never a mechanically shortened adult form. The minor has their own consent block (the minor's consent is required in addition to the legal representative's); it must not read as a mere acknowledgement of having received information. In a coordinated form, each person's decision and signature are clearly separate.
- **Children under 7:** a short, simple information sheet where the child can understand it; no legal consent block for the child.
- **Legal representative of a minor:** full information for the legal decision, including the child's or adolescent's right to refuse and future research choices; number of representative signature blocks per the verified legal basis (DECISIONS.md D9).
- **Adults unable to consent:** participant information adapted to capacity; a participant acknowledgement or signature block only where it reflects the participant's role and capacity. The representative document explains that the decision follows the participant's presumed intent.
- **Healthy volunteers:** states clearly that participation is research and not treatment; risk wording not softened.
- **Impartial witness:** witness attestation and signature block (witness signs and dates); participant or representative block where they can sign; interviewer block; the witness is never labelled as the decision-maker. How the information was communicated is recorded.
- **Emergency enrolment:** the later information separates what has already happened from what happens next; refusal of continuation is a real choice.
- **Pregnant partner:** addressed to the partner; medical-record access and infant data stated clearly; no promise of direct sponsor access to the national Health Information System.
