# Ireland document styling

Applies on top of `content/styles/base.md`. Sources: the Ireland guides (patient information and consent requirements, patient-facing document QC checklist, impartial-witness, limited-capacity and minor guides; all reviewed 23 September 2026) and the `styling_instructions` of each Irish participant template (CTIS_IE set, `IE_13 participant_facing_templates_10082026.txt`). Where they are stricter than the base style, these rules win.

## Rules for all Irish participant documents

- **Language:** English only (plain Irish English for the intended participants); no other language replaces the English submission set.
- **Site letterhead and info table** as in the base style (the Ireland guides add no letterhead rule; site-specific contact details and local procedures must be complete). The info table fields are those listed in each template's `info_table` (trial title, short title, protocol number with version and date, EU trial number, sponsor with full address, principal investigator, site name and address, study identification number, co-investigators, data controller(s), Data Protection Officer).
- **Running footer** on every page as given in each template's `footer`: `[document name] - [population label] - version [x] - [date]` and `Protocol number: [..] | EU trial number: [..]`, plus page "Page x of y" (English replaces the base style's "Seite x von y"). The footer is consistent across the set and never shows editing comments or internal filenames.
- **Title, version and date** distinct for each document and matching its CTIS entry; clean and tracked versions clearly identified where both are supplied.
- **Readable type:** clean readable font such as Calibri, Arial, Verdana or Tahoma at about 12 point (templates; stricter than the base 11 pt); adequate spacing; short sections and paragraphs; no block capitals or unnecessary legalistic text.
- **Headings** as participant-facing questions; headings, lists, spacing and page numbering support navigation; real Word headings with a meaningful reading order.
- **Tables or simple diagrams/flow charts** where they make visits, treatment groups or procedures easier to understand; tables never hide essential information.
- **Accessible output:** searchable source files (Word or original PDF), selectable text, navigable headings and lists; no image-only scans (NREC-CT).
- **Required acknowledgements** as a clear list with one checkbox beside each applicable acknowledgement and the final dated signature; no Yes/No choices or initials for required acknowledgements.
- **Optional choices** only for genuinely optional components, each with its own Yes/No choice (separate tick boxes, initials or signature) that can be declined independently; optional confirmation or signature presented separately from the main consent signature; never pre-ticked; no bundled future-use permission.
- **Signature blocks** with separate fields for printed name, date and signature (time where needed) for the participant or representative and the person conducting consent. Guardian, legally designated representative, witness and interpreter blocks appear only in the versions that use them.
- **Legally designated representative blocks** identify the representative, their relationship or role, the person taking consent and the date.
- **Witness version** fields: participant or representative name and approved mark or signature method; witness name, signature and date; witness confirmation of impartiality and attendance for the full discussion; reason the witness was used; person conducting consent, signature and date; interpreter details and attestation where a separate interpreter is used.
- **Assent documents** (children, adolescents, adults lacking capacity): the assent or willingness record is visually distinct from optional choices; young children are not asked to sign adult legal or data-protection declarations.
- **Consent form** starts on a new page (base style; the templates mark it with a section break).

## Template-specific rules

Verbatim from each template's `styling_instructions`.

### Study Participant Information Leaflet and Consent Form – Adult Patients (`L1_SIS and ICF_main_IE_en.json`)

Use clear participant-facing question headings and short sections. Use a clean readable font such as Calibri, Arial, Verdana or Tahoma at about 12 point. Avoid block capitals and unnecessary legalistic text. Use tables or simple flow charts when they improve understanding. Present required acknowledgements as a clear list with one checkbox beside each applicable acknowledgement and the final dated signature. Do not use Yes/No choices or initials for required acknowledgements. Use Yes/No choices only for genuinely optional components. Where optional components are included, present their confirmation/signature separately from the main consent signature. Use separate fields for printed name, date and signature.
